## Step Inside the Camera Ring

Imagine shrinking down until you are small enough to stand inside a camera lens. Around you, glass curves like the walls of a tiny crystal cave. Light pours in from the front, bends through layer after layer of polished glass, and lands softly on a sensor waiting in the dark. Now multiply that feeling by three, because that is exactly where the new Oppo Find X10 series wants to take your imagination.

On 21 October, in Mallorca, Spain, Oppo lifted the cover off its Find X10 series for the whole world. The phones did not arrive quietly. They arrived with a camera system so bold that the company designed the entire back of the phone around it — a sculpted piece of glass that flows smoothly into the camera bump, as if the whole phone grew around those lenses like a tree growing around a stone.

The star of the show is easy to spot. The series carries up to three cameras, and each one of them is a 200-megapixel camera. Not one big camera helped by two small ones. Three giants, standing side by side. And watching over them is a name that camera lovers have respected for decades: Hasselblad.

## Why Three 200-Megapixel Cameras Change Everything

A megapixel is simply one tiny dot of a picture. Two hundred million of those dots in a single photograph means the picture holds an enormous amount of detail. You can zoom into a corner of the photo later — a face in the back row of a group picture, the words on a distant signboard, the pattern on a butterfly's wing — and the detail is still there, waiting for you.

With three such cameras working together, the phone can cover different distances and different jobs without weak links. One camera can handle the wide scene in front of you. Another can reach far away subjects. Another can get close and personal. Because all three carry the same huge resolution, switching between them does not feel like jumping from a sharp picture to a blurry one. The quality stays strong as you move from lens to lens.

Think of it like a cricket team where the opener, the middle order and the finisher are all star players. There is no weak spot for the other side to attack. That is the idea Oppo is chasing here: no matter which camera you pick, you are holding one of the best.

Honestly, this is the part of the phone I keep coming back to in my mind — most phones give you one great camera and two helpers, and a phone that refuses to include a single weak camera feels like a promise kept.

## The Hasselblad Touch and That Orange Ring

Look at the Pro Max in Luna White and you will notice a thin orange ring around the camera area. That ring is Hasselblad's signature — a small line of colour that carries a very big reputation. Hasselblad is a legendary camera maker whose cameras have travelled from studios to the surface of the Moon, and Oppo has brought its camera technology into the Find X10 series.

What does that mean for an ordinary person taking photos at a birthday party? It means the colours, the skin tones and the light in your pictures are tuned by people who have spent their whole lives thinking about how a photograph should feel. Phone cameras sometimes make colours look loud and artificial. The Hasselblad approach is famous for the opposite — natural, calm, true-to-life colours that look like the moment you remember.

The design team clearly wanted the camera to be seen, not hidden. The back of the phone is made from one piece of sculpted glass, and instead of a sharp edge where the camera bump begins, the glass rises and flows into the bump in one smooth curve. On the Pro Max, the camera island has a matte silver finish with fine knurled edges inspired by classic cameras — the kind of textured grip you would feel on an old film camera's dials. It feels premium in photos, and it is meant to feel premium in the hand too.

## Colours: Pick Your Personality

A phone this bold needs colours to match, and Oppo has split them by model.

The standard Find X10 comes in Glacier Blue, a cool icy shade; Glow Coral, a warm colour with energy in it; and Mist Grey, soft and quiet for people who like their style calm.

The Pro Max steps up with Titanium Grey, which sounds exactly like what it is — serious, strong and businesslike — and Luna White, the pale moonlit finish that shows off that famous orange ring the best.

It is a small thing, but colour line-ups like this tell you who a phone is for. The standard model gets the playful shades. The Pro Max gets the boardroom shades. Oppo knows its buyers well.

## A Battery Built Like a Marathon Runner

Now let us pull back from the cameras and look at the part of the phone you will feel every single day: the battery.

The Find X10 series carries an 8,000mAh silicon-carbon battery. To understand that number, know that many famous flagship phones carry far smaller batteries and still call themselves all-day phones. Oppo claims this one can last up to two days on a single charge.

Two days changes your habits more than you would expect. The charger stops living in your pocket. The fear of a red battery icon at dinner disappears. A long bus journey, a wedding that runs from morning to midnight, a day of taking hundreds of photos with those three giant cameras — the battery is built to take it all in stride.

Even better is the promise about its old age. Oppo claims the battery will still hold 80 percent of its capacity after five years of regular use. Batteries normally grow tired with time, holding less charge every year, and that slow decline is one of the biggest reasons people replace their phones. A battery designed to stay healthy for five years means the phone is built to be kept, not thrown away. Silicon-carbon is the newer battery technology making numbers like this possible, packing more energy into the same space than the batteries of a few years ago could manage.

## The Brain: Dimensity 9600 Pro

Every flagship needs a brain worthy of its body, and the Pro Max carries MediaTek's Dimensity 9600 Pro chipset. Oppo confirmed this pairing at MediaTek Tech Day in Delhi on 6 October, before the global launch — a sign of how proud both companies are of it.

The chip is built on a 2nm process. That tiny number describes how small the pathways inside the chip are, and smaller pathways generally mean a chip can work faster while using less power and producing less heat. The Find X10 Pro Max is among the very first phones in the world to use this chip, which puts it right at the front of the line for the next generation of phone performance.

This matters more than it sounds, because those 200-megapixel cameras produce huge photos, and huge photos need serious processing power. The same goes for the phone's AI features — they need a fast brain to feel instant instead of slow. Oppo and MediaTek also jointly developed additional technology specifically for this series, which suggests the chip and the phone were shaped around each other rather than simply plugged together at the end.

## ColorOS 17 and the AI Mind Pilot

The software running the show is ColorOS 17, Oppo's latest version of its Android-based system, with a refreshed interface and a set of new AI-powered features.

The one with the most interesting name is the AI Mind Pilot, which includes an Agent Mode. The idea behind features like this is a phone that does not just wait for your commands but helps organise your digital life — connecting with your files and photos and working across them intelligently, so the phone behaves a little less like a tool and a little more like an assistant.

This is where the smartphone world is heading, and the Find X10 series is clearly designed to be part of that journey, with the powerful new chip supplying the muscle the AI needs.

## A Launch That Meant Business

Launching a phone series is a statement, and Oppo chose to make this one on a global stage in Mallorca, Spain, on 21 October. A global debut — rather than a quiet release in one country — tells you the company sees the Find X10 series as a worldwide flagship, a phone meant to stand in the same conversation as the biggest names in the industry.

When you add it all together, the message is clear. Start with three 200-megapixel cameras tuned with Hasselblad. Wrap them in flowing sculpted glass. Feed them with an 8,000mAh battery that promises two days of life and five years of health. Give the top model one of the first 2nm chips ever put in a phone. Then dress it in colours from Glacier Blue to Luna White, and let smart software run the whole thing.

That is not a phone playing safe. That is a phone picking a fight with the very best — and bringing a very big camera ring to the fight.
