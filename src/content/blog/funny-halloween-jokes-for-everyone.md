The pumpkins were carved, the lights were orange, and the party had reached that quiet moment when everyone was waiting for someone to do something fun. Then my cousin stood up, cleared his throat in a very serious way, and said, "Why did the ghost bring a ladder to the party?"

Nobody knew. He grinned and said, "Because he wanted to reach the high spirits!"

The whole room laughed, and just like that, the party was alive again. That is the quiet magic of a good Halloween joke. It costs nothing, it needs no setup, and it works for a five-year-old and a grandmother at the same table. You do not need a costume, a stage, or a single spooky prop. You only need one silly line and a straight face.

## Why jokes make Halloween better

Halloween can feel a little scary for younger children. The masks are big, the sounds are strange, and the dark comes early. A joke breaks that tension in seconds. It tells a child that the spooky stuff is play, not danger. It also gives shy guests something easy to say. A person who would never dance at a party will happily tell a joke, because a joke is short and everyone knows when to laugh.

I think a good joke is the cheapest decoration at any party. You cannot hang it on a wall, but people remember it long after the balloons have popped.

## Pumpkin jokes

Pumpkins are the friendliest stars of Halloween, so they make the easiest jokes:

- Why did the pumpkin sit in the sun all afternoon? It wanted to get a little plumper.
- What is a pumpkin's favourite sport? Squash, of course.
- Why did the pumpkin feel so proud? It had finally made the big patch.
- What do you call a pumpkin that loves to sing? A punk-rock melon. Well, almost.

These work best when you say them slowly and let people guess the answer first. Children love shouting out their own guesses, and half the fun is hearing a wrong answer that is funnier than the real one.

## Ghost jokes

Ghosts are supposed to be scary, but in jokes they are mostly polite and a little clumsy:

- Why do ghosts make terrible liars? Because you can see right through them.
- What does a ghost eat for lunch? A boo-rito.
- Why did the ghost fail its driving test? It kept going through red lights.
- What is a ghost's favourite room in the house? The living room, naturally.

The trick with ghost jokes is the voice. Say the ghost's lines in a soft, wobbly voice, and even an old joke feels brand new.

## Witch and skeleton jokes

Witches and skeletons have been Halloween favourites for years, and they carry some of the best wordplay:

- Why did the witch take a taxi to the party? Her broom was in the shop.
- What do you call a witch who lives at the beach? A sand-witch.
- Why did the skeleton stay so calm during the storm? Nothing ever got under his skin.
- Why don't skeletons argue with each other? They simply do not have the guts.
- What did the skeleton order at the restaurant? Spare ribs.

If you are telling these to small children, keep the skeleton jokes gentle and skip anything that sounds even slightly mean. The goal is giggles, not worried faces.

## Vampire and bat jokes

For older children and adults, a couple of vampire and bat jokes round out the set:

- Why did the vampire join an art class? He wanted to learn how to draw.
- What do bats sing when they are happy? Anything, as long as it is in the right key. They are very musical, or so the bats tell me.
- Why did the bat refuse to share its umbrella? It wanted to hang out alone.

## How to tell a joke well

A joke is only as good as its telling. Pause just before the punchline and look around the room. Let people lean in a little. Then say the last line clearly and simply. Do not explain the joke afterwards, because explaining a joke is like opening a present for someone else. It takes the surprise away.

If a joke falls flat, smile and move on. Every comedian has quiet nights, and one flat joke only makes the next laugh bigger. You can also hand the job to someone else. Ask, "Does anyone have a Halloween joke?" and you will be surprised how quickly hands go up.

## Save these for the big night

Keep two or three of these jokes in your pocket for trick-or-treat night. When you meet neighbours at the door, a quick joke makes the visit warmer than a simple hello. At school parties and office parties, jokes fill the awkward gaps between games. And if you are hosting, start the evening with one joke. It tells your guests that this is a night for fun, not fear.

So pick your favourites from this list, practise them once in front of a mirror, and get ready. Halloween only comes once a year, but a joke that makes a child laugh can last in their memory for a very long time. Happy joking, and may your pumpkins be plump and your punchlines be sharp.
