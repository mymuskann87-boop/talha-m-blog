Step inside. The lights are low. A thin white sheet hangs from the ceiling like a ghost that forgot to leave. Somewhere, a clock ticks a little too slowly. Nobody spent a fortune on this room. It was built from old sheets, paper, string, and one very good idea: darkness does most of the work for you.

A haunted house is not about expensive props. It is about what visitors cannot quite see.

## Start With Darkness

Cover bright lamps with thin coloured cloth, orange or red, and switch off the main lights. Use candles in safe holders or small battery lights instead. Shadows stretch across the walls, and every ordinary object suddenly looks strange. In my view, good lighting matters more than any decoration you can buy, because it changes the whole feeling of a room in seconds.

## The Entrance Sets The Mood

Hang strips of black cloth or torn paper over the doorway so visitors must push through them to enter. It feels strange to walk through something you cannot see past, and that small moment of doubt is the whole trick. Add a hand-written sign that says "Enter If You Dare" in wobbly letters. A sign made by hand always feels creepier than a printed one.

## One Scary Room At A Time

Do not try to scare people everywhere. Pick two or three spots and make them count.

**The kitchen:** Put a bowl of peeled grapes on the table with a label that says "eyeballs". Guests will poke them, shiver, and laugh at themselves for being scared of fruit.

**The hallway:** Hang paper bats from the ceiling on threads. They sway gently when someone walks past, and the movement catches the corner of the eye. That tiny movement is scarier than any loud noise.

**The living room:** Drape old white sheets over the chairs and sofa. Furniture shaped like ghosts is simple, cheap, and oddly unsettling. Add a rocking chair that moves on its own, pushed gently by a hidden helper, if you have one.

**The garden path:** If visitors arrive through a garden, line the path with paper lanterns made from old jars and battery candles. Put a few cardboard tombstones among the plants with funny names written on them. The walk to the door becomes part of the show, and guests arrive already in the mood.

**The bathroom surprise:** Stick a few paper spiders on the bathroom mirror and leave a rubber hand in the sink. Nobody expects a scare in the bathroom, which is why this tiny touch gets talked about all evening. Keep it gentle, though. A small surprise is funny, a big one is not.

**The story corner:** Set aside one quiet room or corner with a chair, a lamp, and a book of short ghost stories. One person reads aloud while others listen in the dark. A story told slowly, with pauses in the right places, can be scarier than any decoration. This corner also gives guests a place to rest between the louder rooms.

## Plan The Route

A haunted house works best when visitors follow one clear path. Put small arrow signs on the floor or walls so people know where to go next. Start gentle, build the scares slowly, and finish with something funny, like a room full of balloons or a bowl of sweets. Ending on a laugh means guests leave happy instead of uneasy, and happy guests are the ones who come back next year.

## Sound Is Your Secret Weapon

Play a playlist of creaking doors, distant thunder, and slow footsteps from a phone hidden behind a curtain. Keep the volume low. Sounds that are barely heard are far more unsettling than loud ones, because the brain fills in the gaps with its own fears.

## Clean Up In Thirty Minutes

A haunted house sounds like a lot of work to clear away, but it is not. Paper bats come down in one pull of the thread. Sheets fold back into the linen cupboard. Jars and tombstones go into one box marked "Halloween" for next year. In fact, saving everything is the smartest part of a budget haunted house, because next year the same decorations cost nothing at all. Put the box somewhere you will find it, not in the hardest corner of the attic.

## Keep It Safe

A haunted house should be fun, never frightening in a bad way. Keep paths clear so nobody trips. Avoid sudden jumps at small children or anyone who looks nervous. Tell visitors at the door that they can leave through any room at any time. The best haunted houses are the ones where people scream, laugh, and then ask to go through again.

When the last guest leaves and you switch the lights back on, the sheets are just sheets again and the bats are just paper. That is the magic of it. For one night, cheap things and a little imagination turned an ordinary home into a place people will remember.
