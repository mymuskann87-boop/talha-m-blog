A party without games is just people standing around with drinks. Add one good game and the whole room changes. Voices get louder, strangers start talking, and even the shy guests join in. The games below work for children, adults, and mixed groups, and none of them need special equipment.

### Pumpkin Bowling

Line up six empty plastic bottles and paint or wrap them to look like little ghosts. Roll a small pumpkin toward them. The pumpkin wobbles, which is exactly the point. Nobody can predict where it will go, so every turn gets a cheer. Three tries per player keeps the game moving.

### The Mystery Box

Cut a hole in the side of a cardboard box, big enough for a hand. Fill the box with peeled grapes, cold cooked spaghetti, or a damp sponge, and let guests guess what they touched. Eyes closed, one hand in, wild guesses out loud. This game produces the loudest laughs of any party game I know, and it costs almost nothing to set up.

### Costume Bingo

Before the party, make a simple bingo card with squares like "someone wearing a hat", "someone dressed as an animal", or "someone with face paint". Hand one card to each guest. As the evening goes on, players tick off what they spot. The first full row wins a small prize, like a bag of sweets. It is a quiet game that runs in the background and keeps people looking around the room.

### The Wrapped Sweet Race

Wrap a small sweet in many layers of paper. Sit everyone in a circle. One player rolls a dice while the next player tries to unwrap the sweet, but only while the dice is rolling. The moment the dice stops, the sweet passes on. It sounds slow, but the race against the dice makes hands shake and everyone shout. Children love it, and adults get surprisingly competitive.

### Apple Bobbing With A Twist

Fill a large tub with water and float apples in it. Players must grab an apple using only their mouths, hands behind their backs. The twist is simple: tie a few apples to strings and hang them from a doorway instead. Biting a swinging apple is harder, funnier, and much less wet, which makes it a better choice indoors.

### Freeze Dance, Monster Edition

Play music and let everyone dance like monsters, zombies, or witches. When the music stops, everyone must freeze in the silliest pose they can hold. Anyone who wobbles is out. The last frozen monster wins. This game needs no setup at all, and it works beautifully as a warm-up at the start of the party.

### The Mummy Wrap Race

Split guests into pairs. One person in each pair is the mummy, the other holds a roll of toilet paper. On the word go, each wrapper must wrap their partner from shoulders to feet as fast as possible. The first team to finish with their mummy still standing wins. Toilet paper tears easily, which is exactly why the race is so funny. Keep a few spare rolls ready, because teams will ask for a rematch.

### Games For Quiet Guests

Not everyone loves loud, running games, and a good party has something for them too. Put a Halloween puzzle or a colouring sheet on a side table. Set out a guessing jar filled with sweets and let guests write down their guess for how many are inside. These quiet corners give shy guests a way to join in without being the centre of attention, and the guessing jar keeps people chatting all evening as they compare numbers.

### Prizes Without Spending Much

You do not need expensive prizes. A small bag of sweets, a funny badge, or the right to pick the next song all work. The real prize is the applause. Announce each winner loudly, clap for them, and let them take a little bow. A tiny moment of fame beats a big gift at most parties.

One last thought, and it is something I believe strongly: the best game is the one where nobody feels left out. Pick games where everyone can play, keep the rules short, and let people join late without trouble. A good host makes sure the quiet guest has as much fun as the loud one. That is what turns a normal party into a night people talk about for weeks.
