The truck is parked outside, the driver is waiting, and the house looks like a warehouse that exploded. Boxes everywhere, half of them unlabelled, and somewhere inside the chaos is the kettle the family will need tonight. House shifting is one of life's most expensive chores, and most of the cost is not the truck — it is the disorder. A move planned box by box costs less, breaks less, and ends with the family sleeping in their own beds instead of on a mountain of cardboard.

Moving house means transporting belongings from one home to another. The price depends on how much there is to move, how far it travels, and how much work the movers do. Every one of those three things is partly in the family's control, and that is where the saving lives.

## Declutter before moving — every box not moved is money saved

The first step happens before a single box is packed: declutter. Walk through every room and ask of each thing, honestly, whether it earns its place in the truck. The broken chair nobody fixed. The clothes nobody wore in a year. The boxes in the storeroom whose contents nobody remembers.

Every box not moved is money saved — saved in packing, in loading, in truck space, and in unpacking time. Sell what still has value, give away what could help someone else, and throw away only what is truly finished. Families are often shocked by how much leaves the house in this step, and the move that follows is smaller, cheaper and calmer for it.

In my view, the cheapest box of a move is the one that never gets packed, because it costs nothing to carry and nothing to unpack.

## Get two or three mover quotes and compare what each includes

The second step is the quotes. Never accept the first mover's price. Get two or three quotes and compare what each one actually includes — packing, loading, transport, unloading, and any extra charges for stairs, distance or large items.

Prices differ widely for the same move, and the cheapest quote is not always the cheapest move if it excludes packing or adds surprise charges later. Ask each mover plainly: what is included, what is extra, and what happens if something breaks? A mover who answers clearly is usually a mover who charges fairly. Write the quotes down side by side, and the right choice usually shows itself.

## Pack yourself where you can

The third step is packing, and it is where the largest saving hides. Movers' packing services cost much because they charge for labour, materials and time. Boxes and tape, bought from a market, cost little — and a family's own hands know which items are fragile, which are heavy, and which box the kettle belongs in.

Pack room by room, starting with the things used least — books, decorations, out-of-season clothes — and leaving daily essentials for the last day. Wrap fragile items in old clothes and towels instead of buying wrapping material; the clothes need moving anyway, and they protect plates just as well. Pack heavy things in small boxes so no box becomes impossible to lift, and light things in large ones.

Packing yourself takes time, usually a few evenings spread across a week. That time is the saving, paid in evenings instead of in cash.

## Label every box by room

The fourth step costs nothing and saves the first night in the new house: label every box by room, and add one line about what is inside. "Kitchen — plates and cups." "Children's room — books and toys." "Bedroom — bedding, first night."

Unlabelled boxes turn unpacking into a treasure hunt that lasts days, with the family opening six boxes to find one kettle. Labelled boxes turn it into an orderly process: kitchen boxes to the kitchen, bedroom boxes to the bedroom, and the essentials box — kettle, cups, bedding, phone chargers, a change of clothes — opened first, so the first night feels like home instead of a campsite.

Use a thick marker and write on two sides of each box, so the label is visible however the box is stacked.

## Move on a weekday or off-season day where movers charge less

The fifth step is the date. Movers, like wedding halls, charge more on the days everyone wants — weekends, month-ends, and the busy moving season. A weekday move, or an off-season day where movers have free trucks, often costs noticeably less for the same truck and the same crew.

Ask the mover directly about cheaper days. The saving can be significant, and the only cost is a day off work or a little flexibility. If the family cannot take a weekday, even moving early in the morning — before the day's heat and traffic — can make the crew's work faster and the day shorter.

## What a cheap move cannot fix

An honest limit belongs here. A cheap move cannot make a genuinely large or long-distance move inexpensive — distance and volume set a floor under the price, and no amount of planning removes it. The steps here shrink the bill; they do not erase it.

It also cannot replace care for truly valuable or fragile items. A family piano, a large mirror, or precious heirlooms may deserve professional packing even in a budget move, because one break costs more than the packing would have. Save on the ordinary boxes, and spend carefully where breakage would hurt most.

## The first night box

Every move needs one box that travels with the family, not with the truck crew: the first night box. Into it goes the kettle and cups, tea and sugar, bedding for each bed, phone chargers, a change of clothes, basic medicines, toiletries, and a torch. This box is packed last and opened first, and it is the difference between a first night that feels like home and a first night spent searching through cardboard in the dark.

Mark it clearly — write "FIRST NIGHT" on every side in thick letters — and carry it in the car if there is one. Families that forget this box spend their first evening buying things they already own, from shops that are still open, at prices nobody would choose. Families that pack it sit down with tea in their new home while the boxes wait patiently for morning, which is exactly where boxes belong. Keep important documents and valuables in the same spirit: passports, lease papers, cash and jewellery travel with the family personally, never inside a sealed mover's box. The first night box holds comfort; the family's own bag holds what cannot be replaced, and both travel close together.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How can I reduce house shifting costs the most?**

Declutter first — every box not moved saves packing, loading, truck space and unpacking. Then get two or three mover quotes, pack yourself where you can, and choose a weekday or off-season day where movers charge less.

**Should I pack myself or hire movers to pack?**

Packing yourself saves the most, because movers charge heavily for packing labour and materials. Boxes and tape cost little, and your own hands know what is fragile. Leave only genuinely valuable or fragile items to professional packing.

**Why do mover quotes differ so much for the same move?**

Because quotes include different things — some include packing, loading and unloading, while others charge those as extras. Compare what each quote actually includes, ask about stairs and large items, and choose the clearest quote, not just the cheapest number.

**How do labels help during a move?**

Labelled boxes go straight to the right room, so unpacking takes hours instead of days. Write the room and a short note about the contents on two sides of each box, and keep one essentials box — kettle, bedding, chargers, clothes — to open first.

**Is moving on a weekday really cheaper?**

Often yes. Movers charge more on weekends, month-ends and in the busy season, when demand is high. A weekday or off-season move with a free truck can cost noticeably less for the same crew and the same work.
