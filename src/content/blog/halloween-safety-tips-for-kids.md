Do your children count down the days to Halloween? Mine certainly do. The costumes come out of boxes, the candy buckets get polished, and the whole house starts buzzing days before the big night. Trick-or-treating is one of the happiest nights of the year for children, and with a little planning, parents can enjoy it just as much.

In my view, safety rules work best when children help make them, not when adults just hand them over. Sit down together before the night, talk through the plan, and let your child suggest a rule or two. A child who helped write the rules is far more likely to follow them. Keep the tone light. This is not a lecture; it is a team talk before a fun night out.

## Walk together, stay together

The first rule is the simplest one: stay together. Young children should always walk with an adult, and older children should stay in a small group with a clear plan. Pick a route before you leave the house, and agree on a meeting point in case anyone gets separated. A familiar street with good lighting beats a new street in the dark every time.

Walk on sidewalks wherever they exist, and cross roads only at corners or crossings. Hold hands with little ones near traffic, and remind older children to look both ways even when they are excited. Costumes can be long and masks can block side vision, so remind children to turn their whole head when they look for cars. It sounds small, but it makes a real difference.

If older children go out with friends instead of adults, agree on check-in times. A short message every half hour, a shared location on the phone, and a firm time to be home give everyone peace of mind. The group should stay on the agreed route and never split up to cover more houses. A group that stays together is easy to spot, easy to find, and much safer than children wandering alone in the dark.

## Be seen after dark

Halloween happens after dark, so being visible matters a lot. Bright costumes are easier to spot than dark ones, and a strip of reflective tape on a costume, a bucket, or a shoe works wonders. Glow sticks are cheap, fun, and children love carrying them. A small torch in the pocket helps too, both for seeing the path and for being seen by drivers.

Check masks before you leave. A mask should never block the eyes, nose, or mouth. If a child keeps lifting the mask to see, switch to face paint instead. Face paint is safer, more comfortable, and often looks better in photos. Comfort matters: a child who can see and breathe easily is a child who can enjoy the night.

## Check the treats before tasting

Every child wants to taste the candy right away, and that is part of the fun, but one quick check first is worth it. When you get home, tip the bucket out and look through everything together. Throw away anything that is open, torn, or looks strange. Keep only treats in their original sealed wrappers.

If your child has a food allergy, this step matters even more. Read the labels together and set aside anything that is not safe. A small swap system works well: trade the unsafe sweets for safe ones you bought in advance, so no child feels left out. Keep the mood cheerful. Checking treats can be a fun part of the night, like sorting treasure after a long hunt.

## Knocks, doors, and polite nos

Teach children to visit only houses with their lights on, and to never enter a stranger's home or car, even if they are invited nicely. A polite "thank you" at the door and then moving on is the perfect visit. If a house looks dark or unwelcoming, just skip it. There are always plenty of friendly doors on any street.

Children should also know it is fine to say no. If a game or a dare feels scary or uncomfortable, they can walk away. No amount of candy is worth a frightened child. Tell them clearly: if anything feels wrong, find an adult right away. Children who know this feel braver, not more afraid, because they know they have a plan.

A few last things to pack: a phone with charge for the adult, a bottle of water, and a spare bag in case a bucket breaks. Wear comfortable shoes, because Halloween walking goes on longer than anyone plans. And when the night ends, count the treats together, pick a favourite, and save the rest for later. A safe night is a night everyone remembers fondly, and next year your children will count down the days all over again.
