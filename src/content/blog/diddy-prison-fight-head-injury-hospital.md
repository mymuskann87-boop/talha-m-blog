An ambulance rolled out of a New Jersey prison in the dark, at about one in the morning. Inside it was one of the most famous music men on Earth — Sean "Diddy" Combs — holding his injured head. By sunrise he was back behind bars, and nobody in charge was explaining what really happened to him. Two very different stories are now fighting each other in public. One says he simply hit his head on a bed. The other says he was attacked by one of the most feared prisoners in America — and that the whole prison blames him for making their lives harder.

So which one is true? Here is everything that is known so far, step by step.

## The night of the ambulance

In the early hours of Saturday morning, an ambulance picked Combs up from FCI Fort Dix, a low-security federal prison in New Jersey, about 40 miles from Philadelphia. TMZ reported that the 56-year-old was treated at a nearby hospital for a **head injury that was not life-threatening**. He was conscious and speaking, and he was brought back to the prison before sunrise.

His representative gave only a short statement, saying they were aware of the reports and were working to confirm what happened, and that Combs' health and well-being were the immediate concern. No official explanation of the injury was given — and that silence is exactly where the two stories split.

## Story one: he hit his head on a bunk bed

The first explanation came from a source who spoke to Page Six. According to that account, there was no attack at all: Combs simply **struck his head on the bunk bed above him** — the kind of painful accident that can happen to anyone sleeping in a tight prison cell. The source said he was doing much better afterwards.

If that were the whole story, it would be a small piece of news. But a few days later, a second, much darker version appeared.

## Story two: a fight with "Dudus", the Jamaican kingpin

TMZ then reported, citing sources inside the prison, that Combs had been in an **altercation with Christopher Coke**, a Jamaican drug kingpin known as "Dudus". Coke is not an ordinary inmate. He has been in federal prison since 2012, serving a **23-year sentence** after pleading guilty to racketeering conspiracy and conspiracy to commit assault with a dangerous weapon.

Why would the two men clash? According to TMZ's sources, the anger had been building for weeks. In September, NBC News reported claims from fellow prisoners that Combs was living a pampered life behind bars — with people cooking and cleaning for him, and access to forbidden comforts, including a contraband mobile phone, alcohol and special meals. Combs' side has disputed claims of improper conduct.

After that report, prison officials reportedly raided the facility at least twice and **confiscated phones from many inmates — including one belonging to Coke**. Inmates, the sources say, blamed Combs for bringing the heat down on everyone. The alleged fight happened last Friday during Combs' daily break, while he was already being held in the prison's Special Housing Unit. Both men were reportedly kept in that unit for two weeks afterwards.

It is important to be fair here: **neither version has been officially confirmed.** The prison authorities have not publicly explained the injury, and the fight story rests on unnamed sources. What is certain is only this: Combs left the prison by ambulance with a head injury, he was treated, and he came back alive and talking.

## Why is Diddy in prison at all?

For readers who lost track of the story, here is the short version. **Sean John Combs**, born on **4 November 1969** in Harlem, New York City, built Bad Boy Records and helped shape the sound of hip-hop in the 1990s. His mother, Janice Combs, raised him largely on her own after his father, Melvin Earl Combs, was killed in 1972, when Sean was just two years old. He became a producer, rapper and businessman worth hundreds of millions, and a father of seven children.

His fall came in a New York courtroom. In **July 2025**, a jury convicted him on **two counts of transportation to engage in prostitution**. The same jury **acquitted him of the far more serious charges** of sex trafficking and racketeering conspiracy. He was sentenced to **50 months** — a little over four years — and sent to Fort Dix.

His legal fight is not over. In April 2026, his lawyers argued at an appeals hearing that the sentencing judge had unfairly counted conduct linked to charges the jury had thrown out. That appeal could still shorten his time inside.

## A record of trouble behind bars

The hospital trip is only the latest entry in a growing list of prison problems. TMZ reported that his disciplinary record includes **fights on 31 July and 30 September**, another major violation on 1 October, and two minor phone violations. The punishments were heavy: he **lost 41 days of good-time credit** — the days that help a prisoner get out early — and **400 days of commissary privileges**, the prison shop access inmates rely on for snacks and small comforts.

The cost is already visible on the calendar. His expected release date was first **21 January 2028**; it has now been pushed back to **2 March 2028**.

So the man who once hosted the most glamorous parties in America is now losing days, losing privileges, and spending his nights in a special housing unit — while two versions of one frightening night compete for the truth. His team says his health is the priority. The prison has said almost nothing. And until someone in authority speaks, the bunk bed and the kingpin will both remain suspects in the strangest celebrity story of the week.
