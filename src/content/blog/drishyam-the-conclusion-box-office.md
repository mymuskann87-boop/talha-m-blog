Ajay Devgn has a new biggest hit of his career, and it comes from a series audiences already know well. His thriller *Drishyam: The Conclusion*, the third Drishyam film, has become the highest-grossing film of his career — and it did so in just nine days, according to the reported figures.

If big box office numbers confuse you, don't worry. We'll go through them slowly — and be clear that these figures are estimates that keep changing while the film is still running.

## The short version

According to Sacnilk figures reported by Business Today, the film grossed about ₹446.80 crore worldwide in nine days. That includes about ₹103 crore from overseas, and the film also crossed ₹300 crore in India. Week-one reports had already placed it near ₹250 crore in India. Most importantly, it is now reported as the highest-grossing film of Ajay Devgn's career.

## What do these numbers actually mean?

A film's **worldwide gross** is the total money collected at ticket counters around the world. In this case, according to Sacnilk figures reported by Business Today, that total was about ₹446.80 crore in nine days.

Inside that total, about ₹103 crore came from **overseas** — cinemas outside India. The rest mainly comes from the home market, India, where the film crossed ₹300 crore.

One crore is ten million rupees, so these are very large collections. But a gross figure is not pure profit for the makers, because many costs and shares sit between ticket sales and final earnings.

## Why crossing ₹300 crore in India matters

India is the home market for this film, so a strong run here matters most. Many films open well for two or three days and then slow down quickly. Reaching a figure like this means people kept buying tickets well beyond the first weekend.

The week-one picture already looked strong, with reports placing the film near ₹250 crore in India. From there, crossing ₹300 crore shows the film did not rely only on opening excitement. It kept drawing an audience into its second week — usually a sign that people are recommending it to friends and family.

That word of mouth helps a thriller in a special way. Viewers who enjoy the twists often tell others to watch it, while trying hard not to spoil the ending. That curiosity pulls new viewers into cinemas.

## The overseas story: about ₹103 crore

A film's overseas collection tells you how far its appeal travels. According to Sacnilk figures reported by Business Today, *Drishyam: The Conclusion* grossed about ₹103 crore overseas in the same nine-day period. That's a meaningful share of the worldwide total of about ₹446.80 crore, and it suggests the film found an audience beyond India too — something not every film manages, even when it does well at home.

For a third film in a series, this also makes sense in a simple way. Viewers who liked the earlier films already know the characters and the style of the story. When a new chapter arrives, that ready-made audience can show up in many countries at the same time.

## Why a third Drishyam film had a head start

Series films begin with an advantage a brand-new film doesn't have: trust. Audiences already know what kind of experience they're paying for. With Drishyam, that experience is a thriller built around suspense — tension, smart turns, and an ending people want to discuss afterwards.

Of course, a familiar name alone is never enough. Plenty of sequels fail because audiences feel the story has nothing new to say. The reported collections here suggest viewers felt this trip to the cinema was worth it. Becoming the highest-grossing film of Ajay Devgn's career is no small thing for any actor, and doing it with the third part of a series makes the achievement stand out even more.

## How to read box office reports honestly

Here's something every reader should know: box office figures are estimates that keep changing as the run continues. Different reports can show slightly different numbers on the same day, because collections are still being counted while cinemas sell tickets.

That's why this article attributes the numbers clearly — about ₹103 crore overseas and about ₹446.80 crore worldwide in nine days, according to Sacnilk figures reported by Business Today. If you see a slightly different total tomorrow, it doesn't automatically mean someone was wrong today. It often just means the film earned more, and the estimate was updated. The final picture only becomes clear when the film finishes its run.

Will the total climb much further? We don't know yet, and it's better to say that honestly than to guess a final number. What we can say is that the film has crossed ₹300 crore in India, passed the earlier high point of Ajay Devgn's career, and is still going.
