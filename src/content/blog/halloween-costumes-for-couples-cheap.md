What makes a couples costume actually fun? It is not the price tag. It is the moment two people walk in and everyone instantly gets the joke. One person alone in a vampire cape is fine. Two people, one as the vampire and one as the witch who clearly bosses him around, is a story. And the best part is that a story like that costs almost nothing to build.

The secret of cheap couples costumes is simple: pick one idea, split it in two, and let the pair do the talking. You do not need a shop-bought set. You need a theme, two old outfits, and a little bit of paint or paper.

## Six Pairs That Work Every Time

**Vampire and witch.** One black cape, one pointed hat. A white shirt, dark trousers, and a long black skirt from the back of the wardrobe finish the job. Red lipstick becomes the vampire's bite mark, and a green scarf becomes the witch's shawl.

**Two ghosts with a twist.** Two white sheets sound boring until you give them personalities. One ghost wears sunglasses. The other carries a tiny pumpkin bucket. Suddenly the pair has a joke, and the joke is free.

**Pumpkin and farmer.** One person wears orange from head to toe with a green hat for the stalk. The other wears a checked shirt, jeans, and a straw hat. A paper pumpkin face pinned on the orange shirt ties it together.

**Skeleton and mad scientist.** Black clothes plus white face paint make the skeleton. A white shirt, messy hair, and a pair of old glasses make the scientist. The scientist can even carry a cardboard "experiment" bottle all night.

**Cat and mouse.** Black clothes and paper ears for the cat. Grey clothes and round paper ears for the mouse. The chase writes itself, and guests will play along without being asked.

**Chef and burnt dinner.** One person wears an apron and a chef hat. The other wears black clothes with a paper plate stuck on their chest. It is silly, it is cheap, and people remember it longer than any expensive costume.

## The 30-Minute Rule

Here is a rule that saves both money and stress: if a costume takes more than 30 minutes to put together, simplify it. Cut the extra props. Keep the one detail that tells the story. A witch needs the hat. A vampire needs the cape. Everything else is decoration, and decoration is where money quietly disappears.

Old clothes are your best friend. A charity shop or a borrowed shirt beats a brand-new costume every time. I think the best couples costumes are the ones you laugh about while making them, because that laughter shows on the night itself.

## Money Traps To Skip

Skip the matching sets sold as a pair. They often cost three times more than the same look made at home, and they rarely fit both people well. Skip glitter glue, special contact lenses, and anything you will throw away the next morning. Spend a little on one strong piece instead, like a good hat or a cape you can reuse next year.

Also, agree on comfort before the party. If one person cannot sit down in their costume, or the other cannot walk up stairs, the night turns into a chore. Cheap should also mean comfortable, or the joke stops being funny fast.

## Costumes From Things At Home

Before buying anything, open the wardrobe and the kitchen drawer. A black bin bag with the bottom cut out becomes a witch's skirt. A cardboard box painted grey becomes a robot's body for one partner, while the other wears a lab coat and plays the inventor. An old white shirt with a few red paper spots becomes a dalmatian, and the partner in black with paper ears becomes the firefighter who rescued it.

Hats do heavy work. A cowboy hat, a chef hat, a sun hat with a veil: each one turns plain clothes into a character in seconds. Scarves do the same. A red scarf becomes a superhero cape for a child-sized partner, and a striped scarf turns a grey outfit into a prisoner costume with a matching police officer beside it.

Face paint beats masks for comfort and for price. A few lines of black paint make a cat, a skeleton, or a scarecrow, and paint lets you eat, drink, and talk normally all night. Masks fog up, slip down, and hide the smile that makes a couples costume charming in the first place.

So pick a pair, raid the wardrobe, and spend the evening being a team. The cheapest costumes are often the ones people photograph most, because they carry an idea instead of a receipt.
