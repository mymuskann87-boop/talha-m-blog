It is the morning of the party and you have no costume. Do not panic. Some of the best costumes I have ever seen were thrown together in under fifteen minutes, and honestly, in my view, a quick homemade costume often gets more compliments than an expensive shop-bought one. The trick is simple: pick one strong idea and go all in on it.

## The Classic Ghost

An old white bedsheet, two eye holes, and you are done. It sounds too simple, but it works every single time, because everyone recognises it instantly. Cut the eye holes a little larger than you think you need, so you can see properly, and keep the sheet short enough that you will not trip on it.

## The Tourist Who Got Lost

Put on a bright shirt, shorts, sunglasses, a sun hat, and hang a camera around your neck. Carry a paper map. People will laugh the moment they see you, because the joke is clear: a tourist, lost, on Halloween night. Everything for this costume is probably already in your wardrobe.

## The Mummy

Wrap strips of an old white sheet or bandage cloth loosely around your arms, legs, and body over plain clothes. Leave a little mess on purpose. A mummy is supposed to look untidy, so there is no way to get it wrong. Add dark circles around your eyes with a soft pencil if you like.

## The Witch With A Twist

A black outfit, a pointy hat made from folded black card, and a broom from the kitchen. That is the base. The twist is yours to choose: carry a handbag, wear bright red shoes, or hold a cooking spoon instead of a wand. A small strange detail is what makes people remember the costume.

## The Mad Scientist

Wear a white shirt open over normal clothes, mess up your hair, and carry a jar with coloured water in it. Add a pair of old glasses if you have them. The scientist is a great choice for anyone who does not want face paint, because the costume is all in the attitude. Walk around muttering about your latest experiment and people will play along.

## The Scarecrow

Put on an old checked shirt and jeans, stuff a little straw or shredded paper out of the sleeves and pockets, and draw a few stitched lines on your cheeks with a soft pencil. A straw hat finishes the look. Scarecrows are friendly rather than scary, which makes them perfect for family parties where small children are running around.

## The Robot From Boxes

If you have one cardboard box, you have a robot. Cut holes for your head and arms, cover the box with foil, and draw buttons and dials on the front with a marker. Move slowly, speak in a flat robot voice, and beep instead of laughing. Children find the robot voice far funnier than adults expect.

## Quick Makeup In Five Minutes

A little makeup turns a plain outfit into a costume. White face powder and dark eye circles make a ghost or a vampire. A few whisker dots and a black nose make a cat. Green tint on the cheeks makes a witch or a monster. Keep it simple and test one small patch of skin first, because nobody wants itchy makeup on party night.

## Borrow Before You Buy

Before spending anything, ask friends and family what is sitting in their cupboards. One friend's old graduation gown becomes a wizard's robe. A neighbour's spare curtain becomes a vampire's cape. A hat borrowed for one night costs nothing and returns the next day. Most people are glad to lend costume pieces, because they enjoy being part of the joke. A quick message to a few friends can build a whole costume for free.

## Three Rules For Last-Minute Costumes

First, comfort matters more than looks. You will wear this for hours, so pick shoes you can walk in. Second, make sure you can see and breathe easily. No costume is worth a miserable evening. Third, carry your phone and keys in a pocket you can actually reach. A costume with no pockets is a costume that causes trouble.

The best part of a last-minute costume is the story behind it. When someone asks where you bought it, you get to smile and say you made it this morning. That answer always feels better than a shop receipt.
