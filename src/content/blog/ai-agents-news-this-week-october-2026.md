A lot happened in the world of AI agents in the first ten days of October 2026. An **AI agent** is an AI that does not only answer questions, but also takes actions for you, like writing code, booking things or finishing a task. Here is a simple summary of the most important news.

## Anthropic launched Claude Haiku 5.5

On **October 7, 2026**, Anthropic released **Claude Haiku 5.5**. It is a small, fast and low-cost model. The company says it is meant for high-volume work such as sorting information, pulling out details from text, routing requests, and acting as a helper (subagent) inside bigger agent systems. It has a context window of **1 million tokens**, which means it can read a very large amount of text at one time.

Why it matters: when you run many agents together, the cost adds up quickly. A cheaper small model helps businesses use agents at a large scale.

## OpenAI showed an always-on agent called "Dots"

At its **DevDay 2026** event, OpenAI introduced a personal AI agent named **Dots**, described as an "always-on" assistant. Reports from the same time also said that OpenAI is in talks about a new funding round, but these are reports and not a confirmed deal.

## Meta's Muse agent comes to iPad

TechCrunch reported on **October 7** that Meta has brought its **Muse** personal AI agent to a dedicated iPad app, about a month after the phone launch. Another report says Muse can now help with tasks like purchases and reservations. This shows that companies want one agent that works on every device you own.

## Google introduces the Gemini agent

At **Gemini at Work 2026**, Google Cloud announced the **Gemini agent**, which it calls a single, universal agent for work. Google says it can answer questions, handle knowledge work, create images and media, and write and run code, and that it works inside Gmail, Docs, Sheets and other Workspace apps. The idea is that you give it a goal, not step-by-step instructions, and then come back to finished work.

There was also a report that Google started rolling out its new **Gemini 4 Argon** model, with some doubts inside the company about how well it performs in areas like coding. This is an early report, so it is best to wait for independent tests.

## HackerRank launches Chakra

On **October 5**, HackerRank made **Chakra** generally available. It is an AI agent that runs a whole coding interview: it watches the candidate work in a real project, asks follow-up questions and then writes a structured report for the hiring team. HackerRank says Chakra ran about **500,000 interviews** during its beta period.

## IBM Bob goes self-hosted

On **October 1**, IBM launched a **self-hosted IBM Bob**, its AI coding agent. Banks and governments can run it in a fully isolated network. This answers a growing worry about security when AI agents are connected to private code and data.

## Moonshot AI's open-source swarm

The open-source **Kimi K2.6** from Moonshot AI is being talked about because it showed what a swarm of up to **1,000 agents** working together can do. Open-source models like this give developers more choice than relying on one big company.

## What does this mean for you?

- **Agents are becoming normal.** They are now in your phone, your office apps, your hiring process and your code editor.
- **Cost and trust are the big topics.** Cheaper models like Haiku 5.5 reduce cost, and self-hosted tools like IBM Bob try to solve trust.
- **Be careful.** Before you give any agent access to your money, files or accounts, check what it is allowed to do and keep a human in control.
