**Update — Wednesday, October 14:** The rescue operation at the accident site has now been completed, and most of the injured passengers have been sent home from hospital. But a sad confusion remains: different reports give different death tolls. Pakistan Railways' chief executive and Rescue 1122 officials say five people were killed and 49 were injured, while rescue sources quoted by some newspapers put the deaths at seven, including a baby girl of about 18 months. A formal inquiry into the crash will be held on October 17 and 18. The full updated story is below.

Most of the passengers were fast asleep when it happened. It was around 5:15 on Tuesday morning, October 13, and the Rehman Baba Express was moving through the darkness of southern Punjab, carrying families, workers, students and elderly travellers from Karachi towards Peshawar — one of the longest and most loved train journeys in Pakistan. Many people on board had been travelling all night. Some were close to their stop. Others still had many hours to go.

Then, near Sher Shah Bridge in Multan, something went terribly wrong. Coaches of the train left the track with a violent jolt, and the quiet darkness of early morning turned into screams, dust, broken glass and confusion. Within minutes, rescue teams, police, Pakistan Army teams and local people were rushing towards the wreckage, because in those first moments, every single minute can mean the difference between life and death.

This is the story of what happened that morning, how the rescue unfolded, and the hard questions Pakistan is once again asking about the safety of its railways.

## What Happened in Those Early Minutes

The Rehman Baba Express, named after the famous Pashto Sufi poet Rehman Baba, runs between Karachi in the south and Peshawar in the north-west. It is a train that connects the whole country in one journey. According to a Pakistan Railways spokesperson, around 500 passengers were on board when the accident happened near Sher Shah railway station in Multan district, in Punjab province.

The train's driver, Rizwan Abbas, later described the moment to reporters. He said the train was travelling at about 100 kilometres per hour, the signal was clear, and everything seemed normal. But as the train passed through the Sher Shah station area, he suddenly felt a strong push from behind. He applied the brakes and managed to stop the train. When he stepped out of the locomotive and looked back, he saw the horrible scene: the passenger coaches had come off the tracks and were lying scattered beside the line. The driver maintained that the train itself was in working order.

In total, 14 coaches of the express derailed near the bridge, according to officials. Think about that scene for a moment. A train that only moments earlier was full of sleeping people was suddenly standing broken in the fields outside Multan, with passengers trapped inside twisted coaches, calling for help in the dark.

## The Rescue: A Race Against Time

Rescue work began quickly, but it was not easy. Coaches had fallen off the track, and some passengers were stuck inside. Rescue officials said that 13 coaches were cleared one by one, while difficult work continued inside one remaining coach, where at least one passenger was reported to be trapped as workers struggled to reach him.

A relief train and a medical van were sent from Multan to the accident site to help the teams on the ground. Because of the wreckage, train traffic on both the up and down tracks was suspended. Trains that were supposed to travel through Multan were sent on a longer route through Lodhran, Jahanian and Khanewal instead, and railway officials said the down track would be restored within a few hours.

As always in Pakistan, the first helpers were ordinary people from nearby areas — farmers, shopkeepers, travellers on the road — running towards the sound of the crash before the official teams even arrived. Then came the rescue workers, police, railway staff and hospital teams, all working together while families across the country waited for a phone call, praying it was good news.

By Wednesday, officials said the rescue operation at the site had been completed. The bodies of those who lost their lives were being sent to their home areas, and passengers who escaped injury had been helped onward with temporary accommodation, transport and food arranged by the local administration. According to one report, the search for a missing baby girl of about 18 months was still continuing even after the main rescue work ended — a detail that shows how painful the counting after such a disaster can be.

## How Many Died? The Reports Still Differ

This is the most difficult part of the story, and it deserves honesty. In the first hours, different officials gave different numbers, and even a day later the reports do not fully agree.

Pakistan Railways' Chief Executive Officer, Muhammad Hafeezullah, confirmed to Dawn newspaper that five passengers were killed. Rescue 1122 officials said 49 people were injured: 22 of them were given first aid at the spot, and 27 were shifted to hospital. The chief executive added that most of the injured had been discharged, and only seven patients remained in hospital.

However, rescue sources quoted by the Express Tribune gave a higher toll: at least seven people killed, including three women, two men and a young girl of about one and a half years, with more than 50 injured. A spokesperson for Nishtar Hospital told the newspaper that 21 patients were still receiving treatment there, with one person in critical condition.

So which number is final? At the time of this update, it is not fully settled. The confirmed official figure from the head of Pakistan Railways is five deaths, while some rescue sources report seven. What is certain is that lives were lost, dozens were hurt, and the families caught in this confusion deserve a clear and final answer from the authorities.

## The Minister's Visit, and the Question of Resignation

Railways Minister Hanif Abbasi travelled to Multan, visited the accident site to review the rescue work, and also went to Nishtar Hospital to ask about the injured passengers. He repeated that compensation would be paid to the families of those who died and to the injured passengers, in accordance with the Pakistan Railways insurance policy.

At the site, reporters asked the minister whether he would resign after such a disaster. He answered that people should have some patience: the incident must be investigated first, and those found responsible would be held accountable. He accepted that keeping passengers safe was his ministry's responsibility, and he said the ongoing repair work on locomotives, coaches and wagons would be completed by December 31.

Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif took notice of the accident, expressed grief over the loss of life, and directed the authorities to speed up relief work and give the injured the best possible medical care. President Asif Ali Zardari also expressed grief and ordered a transparent investigation to find the real cause of the derailment.

## What Could Have Caused It?

This is the question everyone is asking, and the honest answer right now is: we do not know yet. The cause of the derailment has not been officially established, Pakistan Railways has said, and more details are expected as the investigation moves forward.

Railways Minister Hanif Abbasi has appointed the Federal Government Inspector of Railways, Amir Nisar Chaudhry, as the inquiry officer. The formal inquiry will be held on October 17 and 18, where evidence will be examined and statements recorded. According to Dawn, an initial investigation report by Pakistan Railways suggested that the derailment was likely caused by a fault in a passenger coach — but this is only an early finding, not a final conclusion.

One detail has received a lot of attention. A passenger told reporters that workers had repaired the nuts and bolts of coach number nine near Rohri, a station the train passed earlier in its journey. This claim has not been independently verified, and it is not clear whether that repair had anything to do with the accident. It is important to treat it as exactly what it is: one passenger's claim, which investigators will have to check.

## A Painful Pattern: Pakistan's Railway Safety Question

Sadly, this is not the first time Pakistan has woken up to news like this. In August 2023, the Hazara Express derailed near Nawabshah in Sindh. At least 30 people were killed and more than 100 were injured in that accident, which raised serious concerns about railway safety and maintenance across the country.

Train accidents are, sadly, not rare in Pakistan. News agencies have long reported that the railway system is struggling with old tracks, ageing equipment and decades of low investment. Ordinary Pakistanis still love the train — it is cheaper than the bus for long journeys, more comfortable for families, and full of memories for anyone who grew up travelling between Karachi, Lahore, Multan and Peshawar. But love for the railway cannot replace the hard work of maintaining it.

Every accident brings the same promises: an inquiry, compensation, better safety. The families of the victims will hope that this time the questions are answered fully — what failed, who was responsible, and what will change so another train, on another dark morning, does not end the same way.

### What happened to the Rehman Baba Express?

The Rehman Baba Express, travelling from Karachi to Peshawar, derailed at around 5:15 a.m. on Tuesday, October 13, 2026, near Sher Shah Bridge in Multan. According to officials, 14 coaches came off the track while around 500 passengers were on board.

The reports still differ. Pakistan Railways' chief executive confirmed five deaths, and Rescue 1122 said 49 people were injured, most of whom have now been discharged from hospital. Rescue sources quoted by some newspapers put the deaths at seven, including a baby girl. The final official figure is still awaited.

The cause has not been officially established yet. A formal inquiry led by the Federal Government Inspector of Railways will be held on October 17 and 18.

### What help was announced for the victims?

The Railways Minister announced compensation for the families of those who died and for the injured passengers under the Pakistan Railways insurance policy. The rescue near Sher Shah is over, the tracks are being cleared, and most of the injured have gone home. But for the families who lost someone in those dark early hours, nothing is over. The inquiry on October 17 and 18 must now do its work honestly, because Pakistan's railway carries millions of ordinary people who trust it with their lives every single day. The least they deserve is a journey that brings them home.
