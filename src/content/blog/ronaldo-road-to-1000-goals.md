Eighteen goals. That is the distance between Cristiano Ronaldo and a number no footballer has ever reached. After his goal against Al Faisaly on 23 October 2026, the Saudi Pro League credited Ronaldo with 982 career goals, leaving 18 to reach 1,000. The statistics company Opta disagreed, recording the goal as an own goal by the defender, which would leave the total at 981 and the distance at 19. One goal, two counts, and a road to history that depends on which count you trust. This post explains the road to 1,000 goals in simple words: where Ronaldo stands, how he got there, what the dispute is about, and what must happen for him to reach the number no one has reached before.

## Where The Count Stands

The count depends on who is counting. The Saudi Pro League, which runs the competition Ronaldo plays in, credited him with the goal against Al Faisaly, taking his career total to 982. Opta, the statistics provider used by most broadcasters and by ESPN, recorded the same goal as an own goal by Al Faisaly defender Andrei Girotto, leaving Ronaldo's total at 981. ESPN reported both figures on 24 October 2026, noting that Ronaldo celebrated his 982nd goal, but that the goal may yet go down as an own goal.

This post uses both figures, because both are reported by named sources, and it does not take a side. The road to 1,000 is 18 goals on the league's count and 19 on Opta's count. The difference is one goal, but it matters, because it changes the date when the milestone could be reached and the story of how it was reached. The dispute itself is covered in full in this site's post on whether the 982nd goal was really his. Here, the point is the road: how far he has to go, and how fast he is travelling.

## How Ronaldo Reached 981 Or 982

Ronaldo's career total is built from club and international goals across more than two decades. He scored for Sporting CP in Portugal, for Manchester United in two spells, for Real Madrid, where he scored the most, for Juventus, and now for Al Nassr in Saudi Arabia. He has also scored more international goals than any other man in football history, for Portugal. The total counts official senior goals for club and country, in competitive matches, as recorded by the standard statistical sources.

The 980th goal came earlier in October 2026, in Al Nassr's 3-0 win over Al Diriyah, when Ronaldo scored in the 23rd minute from a rebound. The 981st, on the Opta count, and the 982nd, on the league's count, came against Al Faisaly. The pace is remarkable for a player who is 41 years old. Most footballers retire in their mid-thirties. Ronaldo is still scoring at a rate that would carry him to 1,000 within a season, if he stays fit and keeps playing regularly. That "if" is doing a lot of work, and it is the heart of the road ahead.

## The Scoring Rate: What The Maths Says

To reach 1,000 from 982, Ronaldo needs 18 more goals. At his recent rate with Al Nassr, where he has often scored more than a goal per game across a season, 18 goals could take around 20 to 25 matches. To reach 1,000 from 981, he needs 19, which is one more game at the same rate. The Saudi Pro League season runs from late summer to spring, with around 34 league matches, plus cup matches and international matches for Portugal. If Ronaldo plays most of the remaining matches and scores at his recent rate, the milestone could come in the spring of 2027.

The maths is simple. The reality is not. Goals do not arrive evenly. A player can score three in one game and none in the next four. Injuries, suspensions, rest, and the form of the team all affect the rate. Ronaldo's team, Al Nassr, creates many chances for him, which helps. But the opposition also knows that stopping Ronaldo is the key to stopping Al Nassr, and they defend against him accordingly. The road to 1,000 is not a straight line. It is a sequence of matches, each with its own chances, and the total will be reached when the chances are taken.

## The Age Question: 41 And Still Scoring

Ronaldo was born in February 1985, which makes him 41 years old in late 2026. No player has scored 1,000 goals, and no player has scored at this rate at this age. The usual pattern in football is that a striker's scoring rate falls sharply after 35, as speed fades and injuries accumulate. Ronaldo has defied that pattern by changing his game: he plays more centrally, he takes fewer long runs, he relies on positioning, timing, and finishing, and he maintains a famously strict fitness and recovery routine.

The age question is not whether he can score. He has shown he can. It is whether he can stay fit, stay selected, and stay motivated for the 20 or more matches the milestone needs. A single serious injury could delay the milestone by months. A decision to retire, or to leave Al Nassr, could end the road entirely. Ronaldo has said he wants to reach 1,000 goals, and his contract and his form suggest he plans to keep playing. But in football, a year is a long time, and 18 goals is a long road at any age.

## The Team Around Him

Ronaldo does not score alone. Al Nassr's attack is built to supply him. In the Al Faisaly match, the goal came from a free kick by João Félix, his teammate, which was deflected off the defender. In the Al Diriyah match, the 980th goal came from a rebound, after a teammate's shot was saved. The pattern is consistent: Al Nassr create chances, and Ronaldo converts them. The team's other attackers, including João Félix and Ângelo Gabriel, who scored the late third goal against Al Faisaly, draw defenders away and create the space Ronaldo uses.

Portugal's national team also contributes goals. Ronaldo continues to play for Portugal, and international matches, including qualifiers and Nations League games, add to his total. The mix of club and country goals is part of the count, and it means the milestone could be reached in either shirt. For the fans, the shirt does not matter. For the record, every official senior goal counts the same, whether it is scored in Riyadh or in Lisbon.

## The Disputed Goal: Why It Matters For The Road

The disputed goal against Al Faisaly matters for the road because it changes the starting point. If the league's count is accepted, Ronaldo is at 982 and needs 18. If Opta's count is accepted, he is at 981 and needs 19. The difference is small, but it is not trivial, because the milestone itself is a round number, and round numbers are remembered. The goal that takes Ronaldo to 1,000 will be celebrated as the 1,000th. If the counts disagree, there will be two candidates for that goal, one match apart.

There is a precedent for this kind of disagreement. In 2010, Ronaldo scored a free kick for Real Madrid against Real Sociedad that deflected off his teammate Pepe. The referee awarded the goal to Pepe, but the Spanish sports newspaper MARCA credited it to Ronaldo. The same pattern — a deflection, a referee's decision, a newspaper's different count — has repeated itself in 2026. Football's record-keeping is not as exact as fans assume. Different sources apply different rules, and the total depends on which rules you follow. The road to 1,000 will be counted by more than one counter, and they may not agree at the end.

## What Must Happen Next

Three things must happen for Ronaldo to reach 1,000. First, he must stay fit and keep playing regularly for Al Nassr and Portugal. Second, he must keep scoring at close to his recent rate, which means around a goal a game across the remaining matches. Third, the dispute over the Al Faisaly goal must be settled, or at least acknowledged, so that the milestone is counted clearly when it arrives. The first two are in Ronaldo's hands and his body's hands. The third is in the hands of the record-keepers.

If all three happen, the 1,000th goal will be one of the most celebrated moments in football history. No player has reached it. Pelé's famous 1,000th goal, in 1969, included goals in friendlies and unofficial matches that modern statistical sources do not count in the same way. Ronaldo's 1,000, if it comes, will be counted under modern rules, in official matches, and it will stand as a record that may never be broken. The road is 18 or 19 goals long. The destination is a number no one has reached. The journey, match by match, is what the next months will be about.
