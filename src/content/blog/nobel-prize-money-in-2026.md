How much is a Nobel Prize worth in 2026? The answer is one number, and it comes from the Nobel Foundation itself: SEK 12.0 million for each full prize. That is Swedish kronor, the currency of Sweden, and it is the amount for one prize, not for one person. When a prize is shared, the money is shared too. This post explains the 2026 figure in simple words, shows how it grew over the years, and clears up the most common misunderstandings about what winners actually take home.

## The 2026 Figure In One Line

The Nobel Prize official website states it plainly: "The prize money for 2026 is set at Swedish kronor (SEK) 12.0 million per full Nobel Prize." The key words are "per full Nobel Prize". A prize is the award in one category, such as Physics or Peace. If one person wins that prize alone, that person receives the whole SEK 12.0 million. If two or three people share the prize, they divide the same SEK 12.0 million between them. The amount does not multiply because more people won.

News reports in September 2026 put the same figure in dollars at the time of the announcement: about US$1.21 million per prize, according to Malay Mail, which reported the Nobel Foundation's announcement. The dollar figure moves with the exchange rate, so it is best to remember the official figure in kronor. The kronor figure is fixed. The dollar figure is only a translation of it on a given day.

## A Rise Of One Million Kronor

The 2026 amount is SEK 1 million higher than in 2025, when each full prize was worth SEK 11 million. That is an increase of about 9 percent. The Nobel Foundation announced the rise in September 2026, before the prizes were announced in October. Its director, Hanna Stjarne, said the increase was connected to the 125th anniversary of the Nobel Prize in 2026, and that raising the amount upheld the long-term significance of the prize and made sure the financial part of it kept its value over time.

The first Nobel Prizes were awarded in 1901, so 2026 marks 125 years since the first awards. The anniversary was the occasion for the rise, not a change in the rules. The prize is still paid from the income of the fund Alfred Nobel created in his will, not from a government budget.

## How The Amount Has Changed Before

The prize amount has not always gone up. It stayed at SEK 10 million for more than a decade, then the Foundation cut it to SEK 8 million in 2012 to strengthen its finances. After that it began to rise again: SEK 9 million in 2017, SEK 10 million in 2020, SEK 11 million in 2023, SEK 11 million again in 2025, and SEK 12 million in 2026.

The very first prizes in 1901 were worth SEK 150,782 each. That number looks small today, but money worked differently then, and the figure cannot be compared directly with 2026 without adjusting for more than a century of price changes. What matters for a reader today is the direction of travel: after a cut in 2012, the amount has climbed back up, step by step, and 2026 is the highest figure in this recent climb.

## How Shared Prizes Split The Money

A Nobel Prize can be shared by up to three people. The Nobel Foundation's rules say a prize amount may be divided between two works, or shared by two or three people who produced the work together. In no case may a prize amount be divided between more than three people. That rule is fixed in the statutes of the Nobel Foundation.

In practice, sharing does not always mean equal thirds. If a prize goes to one person, that laureate receives the entire SEK 12 million. If two laureates share it equally, each receives SEK 6 million. If three share it equally, each receives SEK 4 million. But a prize can also be split unevenly when the awarding body recognises separate contributions differently. For example, in the 2024 Chemistry Prize, David Baker received one half of the prize, while Demis Hassabis and John Jumper jointly received the other half, so Baker received one half and the other two received one quarter each. The same structure was used for the 2025 Economic Sciences Prize.

In 2026, this matters for most categories. Medicine had three winners, Chemistry had two, and Economic Sciences had two. Each of those prizes is one SEK 12.0 million pot, divided as the awarding institution decided. Only the prizes with a single winner — Physics, Literature and Peace in 2026 — paid the full amount to one person.

## The Money Is Only One Part

The cash is the part people ask about, but it is not the whole award. Every laureate also receives a Nobel medal and a personal diploma. The medal is made of gold, and the diploma is a work of art in its own right, made for each winner. The prizes are formally presented at ceremonies on 10 December, the anniversary of Alfred Nobel's death. The Peace Prize is presented in Oslo, Norway, and the other prizes are presented in Stockholm, Sweden.

It is worth remembering that the money comes with the honour, not instead of it. Winners are chosen for work judged to have given the greatest benefit to humankind, in the words of Nobel's will. The money is meant to support the winner and to mark the value of the work. It is not a salary, and it is not paid every year. It is a single payment for the prize.

## Does A Winner Keep It All

A winner does not always keep every krona. Reports on the prize note that the amount is paid before any applicable taxes or other deductions, which means the tax treatment depends on the winner's own country and circumstances. This post does not give tax advice, and no single tax rule covers every winner, because winners live in different countries with different tax laws.

History also shows that winners have used the money in very different ways. Some have given it away, some have spent it on further research, and some have kept it as personal savings. The Nobel Foundation does not tell winners what to do with the money. Once the prize is awarded, the money belongs to the laureate, to use as they choose.

## What The 2026 Rise Really Means

A rise of SEK 1 million is real money, but its meaning is mostly symbolic. It tells the world that the Nobel Foundation's fund is healthy enough to raise the prize in its 125th year, and that the Foundation wants the financial part of the prize to keep its value over time, as its director said. It does not change who can win, how winners are chosen, or what the prize stands for.

For readers, the honest summary is simple. In 2026, one full Nobel Prize is worth SEK 12.0 million. One winner takes it all. Two or three winners share it. The figure is up from SEK 11 million in 2025, it is the highest in the recent history of the prize, and it sits beside a medal and a diploma that money alone cannot buy.
