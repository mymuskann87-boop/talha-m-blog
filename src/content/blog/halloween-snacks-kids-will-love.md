The kitchen smells like warm bread and melted chocolate, and on the table there is a plate of little ghosts made from bananas. My nephew once took one look at them, laughed out loud, and said the ghost looked scared of him, not the other way round. That is the whole secret of Halloween snacks. They should make kids giggle first and eat second.

You do not need fancy tools or rare ingredients. Most of the best ideas below use things already sitting in a normal kitchen, and each one takes only a few minutes.

## Ghost Bananas That Take Two Minutes

Peel a banana, cut it in half, and stand each half up on a plate. Push two tiny chocolate chips in for eyes and one small one for a mouth. That is it. The banana is already white like a ghost, so it does the work for you. Kids love helping with this one because nothing can really go wrong.

## Pumpkin Oranges

Peel a small orange and leave it whole. Push a thin green stick, like a piece of celery or a green sweet, into the top. Suddenly the orange is a pumpkin. This trick works beautifully in a lunch box too, because the orange keeps fresh and the child gets a little surprise at snack time.

## Spider Sandwiches

Make a normal sandwich and cut it into a circle. Lay four thin strips of pretzel or breadstick on each side for legs. Add two small round cheese eyes. I think this one wins the most laughs at a party, because the sandwich looks like it might crawl off the plate.

## Monster Apple Bites

Cut an apple into thick slices. Spread a little peanut butter on one slice, place a second slice on top at an angle so the sandwich stays slightly open, then push a few small sweets into the peanut butter as teeth. The open mouth looks funny and a little bit spooky at the same time.

## Mummy Hot Dogs

Wrap thin strips of pastry dough around small sausages, leaving a tiny gap near one end. After baking, add two small dots of mustard or cheese for eyes. Children love unwrapping their own mummy, and these disappear from the plate faster than anything else at most parties.

## Witch's Brew Juice

Pour green apple juice into a clear jug, add a few slices of lime and a handful of gummy worms hanging over the edge. Call it witch's brew and watch the children argue over who dares to drink first. The drink itself is just juice, so it suits every age.

## Candy Corn Fruit Cups

Layer small pieces of pineapple, orange segments, and a spoonful of whipped cream in a clear cup. The yellow, orange, and white layers look just like candy corn, but the cup is mostly fruit. Parents at the party will quietly thank you for this one, because it balances out all the sweets.

## Turn Snack Time Into A Game

Let children build their own snacks. Set out plain biscuits, bowls of icing, sweets, and dried fruit, then let each child decorate a monster face. No two biscuits will look alike, and the table becomes a craft activity as well as food. Put an old sheet or newspaper under the bowls, because icing will end up everywhere, and that is half the fun.

## Allergy-Friendly Swaps

A few children cannot eat nuts, milk, or gluten, and they should not feel left out at a party. Swap peanut butter for sunflower seed butter in the apple bites. Use rice cakes instead of biscuits for the decorating game. Keep one small plate of plain fruit and plain popcorn aside, clearly labelled, so every child has something safe to eat without having to ask. A little planning here means every child leaves happy.

## A Few Small Tips

Keep portions small. Children at a party want to taste everything, so a table full of tiny snacks beats one big dish. Put water and juice next to the food, because sweet treats go down better with a drink nearby. And always ask about allergies before the party starts. A quick question saves a lot of trouble later.

Halloween snacks do not need to be perfect. A wobbly ghost or a crooked spider only makes children laugh harder. The fun is in making them together, sticky fingers and all, and in watching a simple banana turn into the star of the table.
