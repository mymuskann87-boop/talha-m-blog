## Stop Scrolling: This Year Netflix Gives You One New Christmas Film Almost Every Week

Every December the same question comes back, and this year it has a clear answer. If you search for Netflix Christmas movies 2026, you are not looking for a long list of old films you have already seen. You want to know what is new, when it arrives, and which one is worth your evening. Netflix has answered that question in an unusually tidy way. Instead of dropping all its Christmas films on one day, it is releasing them week by week through November and into early December. That weekly rhythm changes how you should plan your watching. You do not need to binge everything at once, and you do not need to guess which film comes first. You can watch one film, talk about it, and then wait for the next one, almost like a small holiday event in your own home.

The core of the 2026 slate is four new English-language films. They were shown together in a teaser in October 2026, and each one now has its own release date. A Dog's Perfect Christmas arrives on November 4, 2026. Tyler Perry's The Gospel of Christmas follows on November 11, 2026. Guarding Stars comes on November 18, 2026. In a Holidaze closes this group on December 2, 2026. Around those four, there is also a Swedish romantic comedy, The Christmas Switch, dated November 12, 2026, and a family competition series for younger viewers, Gabby's Dollhouse: Makers and Bakers Holiday, dated December 9, 2026 in the United States. This post sorts all of that by mood, not just by date, because a date alone does not tell you whether a film will make you cry, laugh, sing along, or fall in love with the characters.

That mood-first approach matters because Christmas films are not all the same, even when they share snow, lights and a happy ending. Some people want a family film that everyone from a grandparent to a teenager can watch together. Some want romance and a little sparkle. Some want comedy that is light after a long day. Some want music and faith at the centre of the story. Netflix's 2026 group covers each of those feelings, but only if you pick the right film for the right night. Watch the wrong one on the wrong night, and even a good film can feel disappointing. Watch the right one, and a simple evening at home can feel like a proper Christmas event.

## For Family Night: A Dog's Perfect Christmas on November 4

A Dog's Perfect Christmas is the film to start with if your Christmas watching is a family event. It is based on the novel by W. Bruce Cameron, the author behind A Dog's Purpose, and it is directed by Cathryn Michon, who co-wrote it with Cameron. The cast includes Dennis Quaid, Milo Ventimiglia, Brooke Lena Johnson, Jennifer Tilly, Kathleen Rose Perkins and Mary Steenburgen, with Thomas Haden Church as the voice of Winstead, a wise old basset hound. That voice is important. The story is told with the dog's view of the family, which gives the film a warmth that a normal family drama might not reach on its own.

The story itself is simple to understand, and that simplicity is its strength. A teenager, her grandfather and his basset hound try to keep their household together at Christmas during a family crisis. The grandfather, played by Quaid, has become grumpy after losing his wife, and he keeps clashing with his teenage granddaughter. When the girl's mother falls ill, the family is thrown into confusion, and a lively corgi puppy may help pull everyone back together. There is sadness here, but the film is built around love, patience and the small moments that hold a family together. It is not a silly dog comedy, and it is not a cold drama either. It sits in the middle, where a family can laugh a little, feel a little emotional, and still end the night feeling hopeful.

This is also a useful film for viewers who worry about animal stories. Reports on the film have noted that the director has confirmed no dogs die in this story, which will matter to many families who found earlier dog films too painful to watch again. Three corgi puppies shared the role of the puppy in the film, which is a small, happy detail to share with children after watching. If you want one film that grandparents, parents and teenagers can all enjoy without anyone feeling left out, start here on November 4. It sets the emotional tone for the whole season, and it gives you something kind to talk about at the dinner table afterwards.

## For Music and Faith: Tyler Perry's The Gospel of Christmas on November 11

If your Christmas is built around music, church, community and faith, then November 11 is your date. Tyler Perry's The Gospel of Christmas is written and directed by Tyler Perry, and it stars Fantasia Barrino and Tank. Tank plays Isaac, a struggling pastor who inherits his family's church in a troubled New York neighbourhood. Fantasia Barrino plays his wife, Rebecca. As Isaac battles doubt, temptation and a difficult adversary, he must rediscover his faith and the power of prayer to save his church, his family and his community in time for Christmas. That is a bigger, more serious story than a simple romance, and it will speak most directly to viewers who want their Christmas film to say something about belief, duty and hope.

The music is not a small extra here. It is the heart of the film. The cast includes gospel and rhythm-and-blues legends such as Shirley Caesar, Yolanda Adams, Fred Hammond, The Clark Sisters, Smokie Norful, Vanessa Bell Armstrong, Ann Nesby, Hezekiah Walker and JaKalyn Carr, with Kirk Franklin involved as a music producer. For many viewers, that line-up alone is a reason to watch. Gospel music at Christmas is not just background sound. It carries memory, church tradition and family history. A film that puts those voices at the centre is offering something different from the usual Christmas romance, and it deserves to be watched with attention, not as background noise while you scroll on your phone.

This film also fits a different kind of viewing night. It is best watched when you have time to sit, listen and feel the story, perhaps with family members who share your faith or your love of gospel music. It is less about a surprise twist and more about a man finding his strength again. If you watch it expecting only light comedy, you may be surprised by its serious tone. If you watch it knowing it is a story of faith, doubt and community, you will find it much more rewarding. In a season full of romance and comedy, this film gives November a soulful centre, and that balance is one of the smartest things about Netflix's 2026 plan.

## For Light Romance: The Christmas Switch on November 12

One day after the gospel film, Netflix changes the mood completely. The Christmas Switch, a Swedish romantic comedy, arrives on November 12, 2026. It is directed by Adam Palsson, and its two leads are real-life twin sisters, Felice Jankell and Happy Jankell, who play sisters with very different personalities who are forced to swap lives in the weeks before Christmas. Out of their comfort zones, both sisters rethink their choices and find love where they least expect it. The film is adapted from Eleonor Sager's audiobook Christmas in St. Anna, and it brings a fresh, European feel to a season that is often dominated by American stories.

Why does this film matter in a list of Netflix Christmas movies 2026? Because it answers a need that the bigger films do not. Not everyone wants a heavy family drama or a faith story every night. Sometimes you want a bright, funny romance that you can enjoy without thinking too hard, with beautiful winter scenes and a happy, warm ending. A twin-swap story is a classic comedy idea, and using real twins makes it feel more natural and fun. It is also a good choice for viewers who love films like The Princess Switch, but who want something new rather than watching the same trilogy again. The Swedish setting adds snow, small-town charm and a slightly different style of humour, which can make the whole evening feel like a small holiday trip.

This is also a good film for a relaxed night with a partner or a friend. You do not need to know anything about Swedish cinema to enjoy it. You only need to enjoy romance, comedy and the simple pleasure of watching two sisters learn from each other's lives. If you are building a watchlist for November, put this film between the heavier titles. It works like a breath of fresh air. After the emotion of the dog film and the seriousness of the gospel film, a light romance on November 12 keeps the season fun and prevents Christmas watching from feeling like homework.

## For Big Romance: Guarding Stars on November 18

Guarding Stars, arriving on November 18, 2026, is the big romance of the season, and it is the film most likely to dominate social media conversation. It stars Leighton Meester as Hannah Brooks, a no-nonsense bodyguard, and Jared Padalecki as Jack Stapleton, a charming action star. When Hannah is assigned to protect Jack over the holidays, sparks fly, secrets unravel, and Christmas becomes much more complicated. The supporting cast includes Andie MacDowell, Noah LaLonde, Walker Hayes, Toby Sandeman, Rachael Ancheril and Ava Max. The film is directed by Elizabeth Allen Rosenbaum, who previously directed the popular Netflix hit Purple Hearts, and it is based on Katherine Center's novel The Bodyguard, renamed for the film to avoid confusion with the famous 1992 film of the same name.

The story works because it mixes two pleasures that Christmas romance fans love. First, there is the fake-relationship setup. Jack retreats to his family's ranch for the holidays and insists that Hannah pose as his girlfriend, so that his family does not learn about the threat against him. Second, there is the slow change from pretence to real feeling. As they spend Christmas together, the guard and the star begin to see each other honestly, beyond fame, duty and fear. Book fans should note two changes from the novel. The Texas setting has been swapped for a ranch in Montana, and the story now takes place over Christmas rather than Thanksgiving. Those changes make the film feel more festive, with snow, ranch lights and holiday family gatherings replacing the autumn setting of the book.

This is the film to save for a cosy, romantic night, perhaps the weekend before Thanksgiving for American viewers, or a quiet November evening for everyone else. It has glamour, humour, tension and a strong leading pair, and it is the closest thing in the 2026 slate to a classic holiday romance event. If you only watch one romance this Christmas, this is the safest and most exciting choice. It is also a good film to watch with friends who love celebrity romance stories, because the cast, the ranch setting and the bodyguard plot give you plenty to talk about afterwards, from the best scenes to the most romantic moments.

## For Comedy and Second Chances: In a Holidaze on December 2

In a Holidaze closes the main film slate on December 2, 2026, and it is the perfect film for the start of December. It stars Maddie Ziegler as Maelyn Jones, a directionless young woman who makes a major romantic mistake during what is meant to be her family's last Christmas at their snowy cabin. She then finds herself trapped in a holiday time loop, forced to relive the same Christmas again and again until she discovers what truly makes her happy. The cast also includes Rob Lowe, Graham Phillips, Elias Kacavas, Jackie Sandler and Patton Oswalt. The film is directed and written by Tiffany Paulsen, based on the novel of the same name by Christina Lauren, and Paulsen previously directed the popular Netflix Christmas film Holidate.

Time-loop stories work at Christmas because they match how the season feels. Many people repeat the same traditions, the same family arguments and the same hopes every year, and they quietly wish they could do one moment differently. In a Holidaze takes that feeling and turns it into comedy and romance. Each repeated Christmas lets Maelyn try a different choice, learn something new about herself and about the people around her, and slowly understand what happiness actually means for her. That is a warm, funny idea, and it is much lighter than it sounds. You do not need to think about complex science fiction rules. You only need to enjoy watching a young woman grow, laugh at her mistakes, and root for her to get it right in the end.

This film is best watched in early December, when the Christmas countdown has truly begun. It is funny enough for a relaxed night, romantic enough for a date night, and thoughtful enough to start a real conversation about choices, family and happiness. It also fits younger adult viewers who may not connect as strongly with the family drama or the gospel film, but who love a modern romance with humour and heart. If you want to end your November viewing with a smile and start December with energy, save this film for December 2 and watch it as your first proper December Christmas film.

## For Little Ones: Gabby's Dollhouse and Licensed Films

Families with small children need a different plan, and Netflix has included something for them too. Gabby's Dollhouse: Makers and Bakers Holiday is a festive reality competition series, not a film, dated December 9, 2026 in the United States. It follows young baker-and-maker duos in baking and crafting challenges that bring the Dollhouse world to life, with guests including Aidy Bryant, Melissa Villasenor and Justin Willman. For preschool children and their parents, this is a cheerful, colourful addition to the season, and it is best treated as a family activity rather than a late-night film. Watch it in the daytime, let children enjoy the baking and crafting, and keep the evening films for older viewers.

Beyond the new originals, Netflix is also adding licensed Christmas films, and those matter for a complete plan. Reports list The Best Christmas Pageant Ever, from 2024, and Christmas with the Coopers, from 2015, among the titles coming to Netflix in the United States on November 1, subject to change and regional variation. The Best Christmas Pageant Ever is a warm family story about the town's most notorious children taking over a church Christmas pageant, while Christmas with the Coopers follows four generations of a family coming together for a chaotic Christmas Eve. These are not new Netflix originals, but they are useful for filling the gaps between the weekly new releases. If you finish A Dog's Perfect Christmas on November 4 and want another family film before November 11, a licensed title can fill that week without repeating the same new film.

The key lesson for parents is simple. Do not try to make every film fit every age. Use the new originals for older children, teenagers and adults, use Gabby's Dollhouse for little ones, and use licensed family films as a bridge between the big weekly releases. That way, no one is bored, no one is frightened by a story that is too serious for them, and Christmas watching stays a happy family habit rather than a nightly argument about what to watch.

Now that you know the films, here is a simple plan you can actually follow. If you love family stories, start with A Dog's Perfect Christmas on November 4, then watch a licensed family film the following weekend, then return for Guarding Stars as a treat for the adults. If you love romance, skip straight to Guarding Stars on November 18, then watch The Christmas Switch as a lighter follow-up, and finish with In a Holidaze on December 2. If you love music and faith, make November 11 a special night for Tyler Perry's The Gospel of Christmas, with good food, quiet time and no distractions. If you love comedy, pair The Christmas Switch with In a Holidaze, and enjoy two very different kinds of laughter, one from a twin swap and one from a time loop.

You can also build your watchlist by feeling rather than by title. For a crying, warm night, choose the dog film. For a singing, soulful night, choose the gospel film. For a light, smiling night, choose the Swedish romance. For a romantic, exciting night, choose Guarding Stars. For a funny, thoughtful night, choose In a Holidaze. For a children's daytime treat, choose Gabby's Dollhouse. This feeling-first method is better than simply watching in date order, because it respects your mood on the night. A film watched in the right mood feels twice as good, while the same film watched in the wrong mood can feel flat, even if it is well made.

Finally, remember that Christmas films are best when they are shared. Watch with family, talk about your favourite scenes, and let each film start a conversation. Ask which character you related to most, which moment made you laugh, and which ending felt most hopeful. That conversation is the real gift of the season, and Netflix's weekly release plan gives you time for it. You are not rushing through five films in one weekend. You are living with each story for a week, and that slower pace is exactly what Christmas should feel like.

## My Take: Why a Weekly Christmas Calendar Is the Smartest Thing Netflix Has Done in Years

I have watched Christmas film seasons come and go for years, and I have seen the same mistake again and again. A streaming service drops ten films on one day, everyone feels overwhelmed, most of the films are forgotten within a week, and only one or two survive in people's memories. Netflix's 2026 plan is different, and in my honest opinion, it is smarter than most people will notice at first. By releasing one major film almost every week from November 4 to December 2, Netflix is not just selling films. It is selling a rhythm, a habit, and a reason to return. That rhythm matters more than any single trailer, because Christmas itself is not a single day of excitement. It is a season of waiting, preparing, remembering and hoping, and a weekly film fits that season in a way that a single binge day never can.

My first judgement is simple. A Dog's Perfect Christmas is the right film to open the season, not because it is the biggest or the most glamorous, but because it is the most human. A dog film that is honest about grief, family tension and love will reach people who are tired of empty, shiny Christmas stories. I believe families need that honesty at the start of November, when the year is ending and many people are already feeling tired. A film that says a family can be broken and still come together, with the help of patience and a small, lively puppy, is not just entertainment. It is comfort. In my view, Netflix understands that comfort is what people actually search for at Christmas, even when they type a simple phrase like Christmas movies into a search box. They are not only looking for a title. They are looking for a feeling they can trust.

My second judgement concerns Tyler Perry's The Gospel of Christmas, and here I want to be clear and respectful. This film will not be for everyone, and that is exactly why it matters. Too many Christmas slates pretend that every viewer wants the same light romance, and they ignore viewers whose Christmas is rooted in faith, church and gospel music. I think that is a mistake, and I think Netflix is right to give those viewers a major film in the middle of November, with real gospel legends in the cast. A Christmas season that only celebrates romance and comedy is incomplete. A season that also celebrates faith, community and the struggle to believe again is richer, truer and more welcoming. I do not think every viewer must love this film, but I do think every viewer should respect what it represents, which is a Christmas that belongs to more than one kind of family.

My third judgement is about Guarding Stars, and I will say it plainly. This is the film that will decide whether the 2026 season is remembered as good or great. Romance is the heart of Christmas viewing for millions of people, and Guarding Stars has the cast, the director and the story to carry that weight. Leighton Meester and Jared Padalecki are familiar, loved faces, and a bodyguard romance with a fake relationship and a ranch at Christmas is a proven, joyful formula. But I also believe the film must do more than follow the formula. It must make us care about Hannah as a person, not just as a guard, and about Jack as a man, not just as a star. If it does that, it will be the film people rewatch next year. If it only relies on glamour, it will be forgotten by January. My hope is for the first outcome, because the source novel and the director's past success give this film a real chance to be special.

My fourth judgement is about In a Holidaze, and this is where I feel most personally connected to the season. A time-loop story is not just a clever trick. It is a mirror for how many of us live our Decembers. We repeat the same mistakes, the same arguments and the same wishes, and we tell ourselves that next year will be different. In a Holidaze gives that feeling a funny, romantic shape, and it lets a young woman learn, step by step, what happiness actually requires. I think that message will land strongly in early December, when people are already making promises to themselves about the new year. A film that says happiness is not found by changing your circumstances, but by changing how you see the people around you, is a film worth watching twice. Once for the laughter, and once for the lesson.

My final judgement is about the whole slate, and about how we should watch it. I believe we should slow down. Do not rush through all five films in one weekend and then complain that Christmas feels empty. Watch one film a week. Talk about it. Let it stay with you. Cook a meal that fits the film, invite someone who would enjoy it, and make the watching itself part of your Christmas tradition. Netflix has given us a calendar that supports that slower, kinder way of watching. The question is whether we will use it wisely, or whether we will waste it by treating Christmas films as background noise. In my opinion, the films deserve better, and so do we. This season, choose with care, watch with people you love, and let each film remind you why Christmas stories still matter, year after year, in a world that often feels too fast and too loud.

One more thought belongs in this judgement, because it concerns the viewers who are often forgotten in these discussions. Not every household celebrates Christmas in the same way, and not every viewer has a large family, a big house, or money for expensive celebrations. A good Christmas film should still welcome those viewers, and I believe this slate does that better than many previous years. A family drama about holding together, a gospel story about a struggling church, a romance about two people finding each other, and a comedy about learning what happiness means are all stories about ordinary people, not only about wealthy families with perfect homes. That inclusiveness matters to me, and it is one reason I would recommend this slate to a wide, international audience without hesitation. Christmas, at its best, is about belonging, and these films, each in its own way, offer a place to belong for one evening.
