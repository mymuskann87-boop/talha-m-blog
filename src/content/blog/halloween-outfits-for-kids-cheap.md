Halloween is exciting for children, but it can be stressful for parents. A shop costume can cost more than a week of groceries, and the child might wear it for exactly one evening. Then it sits in a cupboard, forgotten, while the receipt sits in your memory for much longer.

There is a better way. Some of the best children's costumes cost almost nothing, because they are built from clothes already hanging in a wardrobe, plus one or two small touches that turn ordinary into magical. I think the best kids' costumes are the ones a child actually wants to wear all evening, not the expensive ones that itch, slip, and fall apart by the second house.

## Start with what you already own

Before you buy anything, open the wardrobe together and look for costume gold. A black dress becomes a witch with a homemade paper hat. A striped shirt and a paper eye patch make a pirate. An old white sheet, with two careful eye holes cut by an adult, is still the fastest ghost in the world.

Ask your child to pick three favourite colours and one favourite animal or character. Then build around that. A child who loves cats only needs black clothes, paper ears on a headband, and a little face paint for whiskers. A child who loves space only needs silver clothes, a cardboard helmet covered in foil, and a drawn-on mission badge. When a child helps choose, they wear the result with pride instead of complaints.

## Five cheap costume ideas that work

**The friendly ghost.** A white sheet, cut to the right length by an adult so nobody trips, with eye holes that are big enough to see through clearly. Add a ribbon belt for shape.

**The little witch.** A dark dress or dark clothes, a tall hat made from black card, and a broom from the kitchen. A green hair ribbon adds the finishing touch.

**The brave pirate.** A striped top, dark trousers, a bandana, and a cardboard sword covered in foil. A drawn-on moustache, if your child wants one, gets the biggest laugh.

**The clever cat.** Black clothes, a headband with paper ears, and whiskers drawn with a soft, skin-safe pencil. A tail made from a rolled-up sock pinned at the back completes it.

**The robot.** Two cardboard boxes, one for the body and a smaller one for the head, painted silver or covered in foil. Buttons drawn with a marker and a pair of old gloves finish the look.

Every one of these can be made in an evening, and every one leaves room for your child to add their own ideas.

## The safety bits people forget

A cheap costume still needs to be a safe costume. Keep these points in mind:

- **Length matters.** Trousers, dresses, and sheets should end above the ankle so your child does not trip on dark streets.
- **Seeing matters.** Eye holes must be wide, and masks should never block side vision. Face paint is often safer than a mask.
- **Being seen matters.** Add something bright or reflective: a strip of reflective tape, a light-coloured bag, or a small torch. Drivers need to spot small ghosts early.
- **Shoes matter.** Comfortable shoes beat costume shoes every time. A pirate in trainers still looks like a pirate.

These small checks take five minutes, and they turn a good night into a safe one.

## Swap, borrow, and pass it on

One of the smartest ways to keep costs down is to stop buying new every year. Ask friends and neighbours if they have last year's costumes in a box somewhere. Children grow fast, so a costume that fit one child last year often fits another child this year perfectly.

You can also organise a simple swap with other parents at school. Each family brings one costume, and each child goes home with a "new" one. Nobody spends anything, and the children love the excitement of choosing. When Halloween is over, store the costume in a labelled bag so next year's search starts easy.

## A night to remember, not a bill to forget

The truth is simple: children remember how they felt on Halloween, not how much their costume cost. They remember laughing with friends, knocking on doors, and showing off their outfit to grandparents. No child has ever come home and said, "That was a wonderful night, but I wish my costume had cost more."

So start with the wardrobe, add one fun detail, check the safety bits, and let your child lead the way. The result will be cheaper, more comfortable, and more personal than anything in a shop window. And when your child runs out of the door, proud and happy in an outfit you made together, that feeling is worth far more than any price tag.
