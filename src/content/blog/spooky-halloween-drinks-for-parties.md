The table is covered with a black cloth. A big bowl sits in the middle, fog curling over its edge, and guests keep drifting over just to stare at it. Nobody remembers the playlist from a party. Everyone remembers the drink that looked like it came from a witch's kitchen.

Spooky drinks work because they play a trick on the eyes while tasting completely normal. The fog, the colour, the floating jelly shapes: all of it is theatre, and the theatre is cheap. What follows are four drinks that look magical, taste sweet and fresh, and suit guests of every age.

## Witch's Green Punch

Mix apple juice, lemonade, and a splash of lime cordial in a big bowl. The green colour comes from the apple juice and lime, not from food colouring. Drop in slices of green apple and a handful of frozen grapes instead of ice cubes. Frozen grapes keep the punch cold without watering it down, and they look like little eyeballs bobbing in a potion.

## Ghostly White Lemonade

Blend plain lemonade with a little coconut milk until it turns pale and cloudy. Pour it into clear glasses and add a white jelly ghost on top, cut from a flat sheet of jelly with a small ghost-shaped cutter or a knife. Kids love fishing their ghost out with a spoon. Adults love that it took five minutes.

## Pumpkin Fizz

Stir pumpkin puree into orange juice, add a pinch of cinnamon, and top it up with sparkling water just before serving. The fizz makes it feel like a celebration, and the cinnamon gives it that autumn smell people associate with October. Serve it in small glasses, because it is rich and a little goes a long way.

## Berry Night Punch

Crush mixed berries into cranberry juice, add lemonade, and finish with sparkling water. The deep red colour looks dramatic in a glass bowl, especially with a few blackberries floating on top. In my opinion, one big punch bowl beats ten fancy bottles at a party, because everyone gathers around it and the party gathers with them.

## Dry Ice, Handled Right

Dry ice is what makes a punch bowl smoke like a cauldron, and it deserves respect. Only adults should handle it. Never touch dry ice with bare hands: use thick gloves or tongs, because it is cold enough to burn skin on contact. Never put dry ice into a drink that someone will sip, and never swallow it or drop it into a single glass. Use a large piece in the punch bowl only while the bowl sits in a well-ventilated room, and keep children well away from the bowl itself. When the fog fades, let the piece melt away on its own in a safe spot, out of reach.

Handled this way, dry ice is the safest kind of magic: all show, no risk.

## Setting Up The Table

A drink tastes better when the table looks the part. Cover the table with a dark cloth, scatter a few plastic spiders, and place the bowl in the centre where everyone can reach it. Put a ladle beside it and a stack of small cups, because guests serve themselves more happily than they wait to be served.

Label each drink with a small card. "Witch's Punch" and "Ghost Lemonade" sound far more exciting than "green juice" and "white lemonade", and labels stop guests from asking what everything is. Add a plate of simple snacks beside the drinks, like popcorn or plain biscuits, so nobody drinks on an empty stomach.

Keep a jug of plain water on the table too. Sweet drinks make people thirsty, and water keeps the party comfortable for children and adults alike. Refill the punch bowl before it runs empty, because a half-empty bowl loses its magic fast, while a full one keeps pulling people back. Any punch left at the end of the night can be covered and kept in the fridge for the next day, as long as no dry ice went into that batch. A drink that still tastes good tomorrow is a small bonus for the host.

Set out the bowl, dim the lights a little, and let the fog do the talking. A party drink does not need to be complicated. It needs to look like fun, taste like a treat, and give everyone something to gather around.
