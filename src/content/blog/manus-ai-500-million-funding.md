An AI startup called Manus just got a huge vote of trust, worth more than $500 million.
And it happened after the company stepped away from a much bigger deal with Meta.

If words like "funding round" and "acquisition" sound confusing, do not worry. This article explains the whole story in simple, everyday words.

## The short version

Butterfly Effect, the parent company of the AI agent startup Manus, announced a funding round of more than $500 million. The round came after unwinding an acquisition agreement with Meta that was reportedly worth more than $2 billion. The new round was co-led by Boyu Capital and IDG Capital, with Tencent, HSG and ZhenFund taking part. The money lets Manus keep working independently.

## First, what is an AI agent startup?

Let us start with the basics, because the rest of the story makes more sense after this part.

A normal AI chatbot mostly answers. You ask a question, and it gives you text back. An AI agent goes one step further. It can take actions and carry out a task in steps, not just talk about the task.

Imagine a helpful assistant. A chatbot might tell you how to plan a trip. An agent tries to do the work itself, step by step. A startup is a young company still growing, so an AI agent startup is a young company building this kind of action-taking AI. Manus is that startup, and Butterfly Effect is its parent company — the bigger company that owns or controls it.

## What happened with the Meta deal?

Before this funding news, there was an acquisition agreement with Meta.

An acquisition is when one company buys another company. If that deal had gone ahead, Manus would not have stayed fully on its own path in the same way. It would have become part of a much bigger company.

But that agreement was unwound. The deal with Meta was reportedly worth more than $2 billion.

Put the two numbers side by side and you can see why people paid attention. A deal worth more than $2 billion did not go ahead, and then the company raised more than $500 million on a different path. That is not a small business story. It is a major change of direction.

## What does "unwinding an acquisition" mean?

This phrase sounds technical, but the idea is simple.

Think of two sides who agree to join together, then step back, undo the agreement, and go their separate ways again. That undoing is "unwinding". It does not always mean the sides are enemies — it means the deal is being reversed instead of completed.

Here, unwinding the agreement with Meta left room for Manus to keep working independently, making its own plans rather than becoming part of Meta. Note the careful word, too: the deal was reportedly worth more than $2 billion, which means that is the value reported in the news.

## Who put in the new money, and how much?

Butterfly Effect announced a funding round of more than $500 million.

A funding round is a stage where a company raises money from investors. Investors give money because they believe the company can grow and become more valuable over time. In return, they usually get a share of the company.

The round was co-led by Boyu Capital and IDG Capital. "Co-led" means those two firms were the main leaders of the round, sharing that role. Tencent, HSG and ZhenFund also took part.

The key point is the size and the signal. More than $500 million is a very large amount for a young company, and several named investors took part together — a clear show of interest in Manus staying on its own path.

## Why would investors put big money into an independent AI company?

This is the question many readers will ask. If a huge company was part of a deal worth billions, why would investors now back the company on its own?

There are a few simple reasons. First, an independent company can move with a clear focus, making its own choices without fitting inside a giant company's many other plans. Second, investors may believe it can become more valuable by growing on its own — putting in more than $500 million says they see a future worth backing. Third, AI agents are an area many investors want to be part of, because an agent that can do tasks could be useful in many kinds of work.

None of this guarantees success. Big funding helps a company hire, build, and keep going, but it does not promise every plan will work. What it shows is belief — and for Manus, the practical result is clear: the money lets it keep working independently.
