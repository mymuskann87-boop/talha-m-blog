Every pumpkin starts as a blank face. A child draws two triangle eyes on it with a marker, and suddenly it has a personality. Ten minutes later, it is glowing on the porch, and the child is telling everyone, "I made that."

Pumpkin carving sounds tricky, but it is really just drawing, scooping, and cutting along lines. With a little patience and the right safety rules, even a complete beginner can make a pumpkin worth showing off.

## Pick the Right Pumpkin

Start at the shop or the market and take your time choosing. A good carving pumpkin feels firm, not soft, and sits flat without wobbling. A flat bottom matters more than a perfect shape, because a wobbly pumpkin tips over and its candle falls out.

Pick one with a smooth front, because smooth skin is easier to draw on and cut through. A stem on top is useful too, since it works like a handle when you lift the lid.

Size is up to you. A medium pumpkin is easiest for a first try. Very large pumpkins take longer to scoop out, and very small ones are hard to cut. Medium is the sweet spot.

## Draw First, Cut Second

This is the most important rule in the whole process: draw your design before you cut anything. A marker lets you plan, change your mind, and fix mistakes. A knife does not.

Safety comes first, and it is simple. Adults handle sharp tools, always. Children draw the designs, choose the faces, and scoop out the inside with a spoon. That way everyone has a job, and nobody gets hurt.

To open the pumpkin, an adult cuts a circle around the stem to make a lid. Then everyone can scoop out the seeds and the stringy insides with a big spoon. It feels strange and slimy, and children usually love it. Put the seeds aside in a bowl. They are useful later, and we will come back to them.

Once the inside is clean, tape or draw your design on the front. Simple shapes work best for beginners: triangles, circles, and zigzags. Save the fancy details for your second or third pumpkin.

## Five Easy Designs to Try

**1. The Happy Face.** Two triangle eyes, a triangle nose, and a big smiling mouth. This is the classic jack-o-lantern, and it never looks wrong.

**2. The Toothy Grin.** One big eye, one small eye, and a wide mouth full of square teeth. It looks silly and friendly, and children love drawing it.

**3. The Star Eyes.** Two star shapes for eyes and a small round mouth. When the candle glows inside, the stars shine out, and the pumpkin looks like it is winking at the street.

**4. The Cat.** Two triangle ears on top, two round eyes, and a mouth with whiskers. Cut the ears as part of the lid shape or draw them on the front. Cat pumpkins always get compliments.

**5. The Silly Face.** One eye bigger than the other, a crooked smile, and a nose pointing sideways. Silly faces are the most fun, because there is no wrong way to make them.

Cut slowly and carefully, always cutting away from your hands and body. If a piece breaks off, do not worry. A broken tooth or a chipped ear just gives your pumpkin more character.

## Light It Up Safely

A battery candle is the safest way to light a pumpkin, especially on a porch where children walk past. It glows warmly, it lasts for hours, and there is no flame near dry leaves or paper decorations.

If you use a real candle, an adult should light it and place it inside, and the pumpkin should stay outside, away from curtains, paper, and anything that can catch fire. Never leave a burning candle alone.

For a fun twist, try a coloured battery light inside your pumpkin. An orange light looks classic, but a purple or green light makes your pumpkin stand out on a street full of orange ones.

## Do Not Waste the Inside

Those seeds you scooped out earlier? Wash them, dry them, toss them with a little salt and oil, and roast them in the oven until they turn golden. They make a crunchy, healthy snack, and they taste like a reward for your hard work.

The soft pumpkin flesh can go into soup or a simple mash. Nothing needs to go to waste, and using the whole pumpkin feels good.

I honestly believe the wonkiest pumpkin on the porch is the one everyone remembers. A perfect pumpkin from a shop is easy to forget. A crooked grin drawn by a child, glowing on your steps, is the one neighbours point at and smile about.

So grab a marker, pick a pumpkin, and start drawing. Your porch is waiting for its new face.
