The lights are low. Orange string lights glow along the wall. A bowl of punch sits in the middle of the table with dry ice fog curling over the edge, and someone has just put on a playlist of creaky doors and distant thunder. Your friends walk in, laugh at the paper ghosts hanging from the ceiling, and reach for a cookie shaped like a bat. Nobody at this party knows that the whole evening cost less than one takeaway dinner.

That is the secret of a great Halloween party. Guests remember the feeling, not the spending. A dark room, a few clever decorations, and food with silly names will do more than any expensive display. The best parties I have been to were the ones where the host had fun making things, not the ones where the host spent the most money.

## Turn down the lights

Lighting changes everything, and it costs almost nothing. Switch off the bright ceiling lights and use lamps, candles in jars, or string lights instead. If you have real candles, use them safely in sturdy holders, far from paper decorations and curtains. Battery candles are even better: they flicker like real ones, they are safe around children, and you can reuse them year after year.

Cut paper shapes and tape them over a lamp or a jar and you get instant shadows. Bats, spiders, and crooked trees all look wonderful as silhouettes on a wall. Hang black bin bags cut into strips over a doorway and guests have to push through a spooky curtain to enter. Old white sheets make great ghosts: stuff the top with newspaper, tie it with string, and hang it from the ceiling with a little fishing line.

## Food that looks scary and tastes great

Halloween food should look a little silly and taste really good. Give ordinary snacks spooky names and watch guests smile. Sausages become "witch fingers" with a little almond stuck on the end as a nail. Orange juice becomes "pumpkin potion". Sandwiches cut into triangles become "bat wings". Children love the names, and adults love them just as much.

Keep it simple and cheap. A big pot of soup, a tray of sandwiches, and a bowl of popcorn will feed a crowd without fuss. Add one showpiece, like a cake with orange icing or a punch bowl with floating jelly "eyeballs" made from grapes. Ask guests about food allergies before the party, and label anything with nuts or dairy. A small card next to each dish takes two minutes and saves a guest a worry.

## Games that cost almost nothing

Games are where a cheap party becomes a brilliant one. "Pin the wart on the witch" needs only a printed picture and a few paper circles. A pumpkin hunt works like an Easter egg hunt: hide small pumpkins or wrapped sweets around the room and let guests search. Apple bobbing needs only a bucket, water, and apples, though it gets messy, so put towels down first.

A costume contest costs nothing at all and everyone loves it. Make up funny prizes: scariest, silliest, most creative, and best homemade. The prizes can be a paper crown, a silly badge, or the honour of picking the next song. If your guests are shy, start the games yourself and be the first to look silly. Once one person laughs at themselves, everyone relaxes.

## Music and mood on a shoestring

A playlist is free, and it does half the work of setting the mood. Mix a few spooky sound effects with songs everyone knows, and keep the volume low enough that people can talk. A speaker in the corner and a phone is all you need. If you want a laugh, play a "monster mash" style song when people arrive; it tells guests at once that this party is about fun, not fright.

I honestly believe a small party full of laughter beats a big party full of bills. Invite the people you actually enjoy, keep the guest list small enough that everyone can talk, and do not try to impress anyone. Put the food out early, keep the games short, and leave time for people to just sit and chat. Some of the best party moments happen in those quiet corners, with a cup of something warm and a friend telling a silly story.

When the night ends, send guests home with a small treat bag: a few sweets, a paper ghost, or a funny photo from the evening. It costs almost nothing and people remember it for weeks. Then put your feet up, look at the paper bats still hanging from the ceiling, and enjoy the best feeling of all: a wonderful party that did not break the bank.
