Thirty minutes to the party. No costume. Just you, a mirror, and a small bag of makeup. Do not panic. Some of the best Halloween looks need no costume at all, only a few clever lines and a bit of colour. By the time you finish reading this, you will have four looks you can do tonight, fast, with things you probably already own.

Before you start, two quick rules. First, use only products made for skin, and test a small dab on your wrist first if you have sensitive skin. Second, keep everything away from your eyes unless the product is meant for eyes. Wash it all off before bed with warm water and soap, and never share makeup with friends, because shared makeup can spread skin problems. Safe and simple, then fun.

## The five-minute cat

The cat is the fastest Halloween look in the world, and it always works. Start with your normal foundation or a light powder. Draw a small black triangle on the tip of your nose with eyeliner. Add three whisker lines on each cheek, curving slightly upward. Finish with a flick of eyeliner at the corners of your eyes, like a cat's eyes.

That is it. Five minutes, and you are a cat. If you have two extra minutes, add pointy ears: cut two triangles from cardboard, colour them black, and clip them into your hair. A black top completes the look. Wear it to any party and people will get it instantly, which is the whole point of a quick look.

## A friendly ghost face

A ghost face sounds hard, but it is really just pale skin and dark eyes. Cover your face with a light foundation or white face paint, a shade or two lighter than your skin. Blend it well at the edges so it does not look like a mask. Then darken around your eyes with grey or black eyeshadow, blending outward like shadows.

My own take is simple: makeup should feel like play, not a test. Do not worry about perfect lines. Ghosts are supposed to look a little messy. Add a pale lip colour, mess up your hair a little, and wear a white or grey top. You will look like you floated in from a very old house, and you did it in ten minutes.

## The pumpkin grin

This one makes everyone smile. Start with an orange base: orange face paint, or a warm orange blush blended all over the face. Keep it light; you want a glow, not a mask. Then draw a wide grin with black eyeliner, curving up at the corners like a carved pumpkin. Add a few short vertical lines above and below the grin, like the cuts in a jack-o-lantern.

Finish with a green touch: a small green leaf shape on your forehead, or a green hairband. A green or brown top ties it together. This look is cheerful, bright, and great for photos. It works beautifully for children too, with a parent doing the drawing, as long as the products are gentle and made for young skin.

## Fix the small mistakes fast

Quick makeup means small mistakes, and that is fine. A cotton bud dipped in makeup remover fixes a wobbly line in seconds. If a line is too thick, smudge it softly with your finger and call it shading. If the colours look too strong, press a tissue gently over the face to lift the extra colour. Most mistakes disappear with one soft blend.

Keep a small kit by the mirror: cotton buds, tissues, makeup remover, and your main colours. That way you never have to hunt for anything mid-look. And here is a trick from people who do this every year: do your makeup before you put on your top, so a stray mark does not land on your clothes. Put the top on last, step back, and check the whole look in the mirror.

One last thing: take a photo before you leave. Quick looks often photograph better than long ones, because they are fresh and bold. If the photo looks good, the look is good. Then go and enjoy the party. You got ready in half an hour, you spent almost nothing, and you look like you planned it for days. That is the real magic of Halloween makeup: a little colour, a little courage, and a mirror.

When the party is over, take the makeup off gently. Warm water, a soft cloth, and a mild soap will remove most of it, and a little moisturiser afterwards keeps your skin happy. Do not scrub hard or sleep with the makeup on, because your skin needs to breathe overnight. A calm end to the night is part of the look too.
