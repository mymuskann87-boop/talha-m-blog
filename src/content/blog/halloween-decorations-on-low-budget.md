Walk down any street in the last week of October, and you will notice something. Some houses glow orange and purple, with paper ghosts hanging from the porch, and kids stop to stare. Other houses stay dark and plain. The funny thing is, the glowing house did not always spend more money. Sometimes the owner just used a little imagination and a few things from around the house.

Halloween decorations are not about buying the biggest skeleton in the shop. They are about creating a feeling. A feeling of mystery, a little spookiness, and a lot of fun. And that feeling costs far less than most people think.

## Start With What You Already Own

Before you buy anything new, open a cupboard and look around. An old white bedsheet can become a ghost on the porch. Just drape it over a chair or a ball on a stick, draw two dark eyes with a marker, and you have a friendly ghost guarding your door.

Glass jars are another secret weapon. Put a small battery candle inside a jar, wrap a little orange paper around it, and you have a glowing lantern. Line three or four of them along your steps, and suddenly your entrance looks like a scene from a Halloween movie.

Old cardboard boxes can turn into tombstones. Cut a rounded shape, paint it grey, and write a funny name on it with a black marker. Names like "Mr. Bones" or "Sleepy Sam" make children laugh instead of feeling scared. Place them in the garden, lean them against the wall, and your yard becomes a tiny graveyard.

## Paper, Cardboard, and a Little Paint

Paper is the cheapest decoration material in the world, and it works wonders at Halloween. Cut bat shapes from black paper and stick them on the wall or the window. Cut a spider web shape from white paper and hang it in a corner. Children love helping with this part, and their slightly wonky bats look more charming than anything from a shop.

Orange and black balloons cost very little, and they change a room at once. Blow up five or six, draw pumpkin faces on the orange ones with a marker, and scatter them around. If you have a black marker and a steady hand, you can draw a spider web on a white balloon too.

Pumpkins themselves are decorations, even before you carve them. A row of plain pumpkins on the steps looks festive and warm. Paint a face on one instead of carving it, and it will last much longer.

## Lights Do the Heavy Lifting

If there is one place to spend a small amount of money, it is lights. One string of orange or purple fairy lights can transform a whole porch. Wrap them around the door frame, along the fence, or around a tree, and the whole house starts to glow.

I think a home decorated with love beats a shop-bought display every single time. A single warm string of lights, placed with care, feels more inviting than ten expensive plastic monsters standing in a row.

Battery candles are safer than real candles, especially if children will be running around. They flicker like real flames, they last for hours, and you can put them inside jars, bottles, and paper bags without worry. For paper bag lanterns, fold the top of a brown paper bag down, cut a few small shapes in the sides, and drop a battery candle inside. The light shining through the cut-out shapes looks magical.

## The Porch Is Your Stage

You do not need to decorate the whole house. Guests and trick-or-treaters only really see the entrance. So put your best effort there.

Hang one ghost from the porch roof. Put two glowing jars on the steps. Place a pumpkin on each side of the door. Add a welcome sign that says something playful, like "Enter if you dare!" on a piece of cardboard. That is enough. A small, well-decorated porch looks better than a whole house covered in random things.

A doormat can join the fun too. Lay a piece of black cloth over your normal doormat, or paint a simple spider web on a plain mat. Small touches like this make visitors smile before they even knock.

## Keep It Safe and Simple

Spooky is fun. Dangerous is not. Keep paths and steps clear so nobody trips over wires or decorations. Use battery lights instead of real flames near paper, fabric, and dry leaves. If you hang things from above, make sure they are high enough that nobody walks into them.

When the night is over, pack your decorations in a box and label it. Next year, you will open that box and find most of what you need already waiting. That is the real secret of a low budget Halloween: spend once, reuse for years, and add one or two new handmade pieces each year.

So this year, before you head to the shops, look around your home. A sheet, a jar, a box, and a string of lights might be all you need to make your house the one children remember on the street.
