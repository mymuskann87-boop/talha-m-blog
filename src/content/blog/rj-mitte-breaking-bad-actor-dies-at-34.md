Some television faces stay with you forever. RJ Mitte was one of those faces. For five seasons of *Breaking Bad*, he played Walter White Jr. — the teenage son in the middle of a family that was slowly falling apart — and he played the part with a warmth and honesty that viewers never forgot. On Monday, October 12, 2026, Mitte died at his home in Brownsville, Texas. He was only 34 years old. The news has left fans of the show, and the many people he inspired far beyond it, in deep sadness.

## The short version

RJ Mitte, the actor best known as Walter White Jr. in the hit AMC series *Breaking Bad*, died on Monday, October 12, 2026, at his home in Brownsville, Texas, aged 34. His death was confirmed by the talent management agency that represented him. Brownsville police say no foul play is suspected at this time, and their investigation is ongoing. No official cause of death has been announced. Mitte, who lived with cerebral palsy, was also a well-known disability rights advocate, and warm tributes have poured in from his *Breaking Bad* family, including Bryan Cranston.

## What we know about his passing

According to reports, Brownsville police received an emergency call around midday on Monday. When officers arrived at the home, Mitte was already deceased. Cameron County Justice of the Peace Judge Cyndi Hinojosa said he was declared dead at 1:32 p.m. local time, as reported by USA TODAY. Police said no foul play is suspected at this time, and the investigation is ongoing, CNN reported. Officials have not announced any cause of death.

In a statement, the talent management agency that represented Mitte confirmed his death and remembered him as a "beloved actor, philanthropist, and passionate disability rights advocate." Those three descriptions — actor, giver, fighter for others — sum up the life he lived in a short 34 years.

## A young life that began with a challenge

RJ Mitte was born Roy Frank Mitte III on August 21, 1992, in Jackson, Mississippi, in the United States. At the age of three, he was diagnosed with cerebral palsy, a condition that affects movement and muscle control. For many children, such a diagnosis can quietly close doors. For Mitte, it became the start of a very different story.

In 2006, he moved to Los Angeles to find acting work. Think about what that means: a teenager living with a disability, travelling across the country to enter one of the toughest industries in the world — an industry that, at the time, rarely made space for performers like him. He was not looking for sympathy. He was looking for a chance. And just two years later, that chance arrived in a way nobody could have predicted.

## Becoming Walter White Jr.

In 2008, Mitte joined the cast of a new AMC drama called *Breaking Bad*. He played Walter White Jr., the son of chemistry teacher turned drug maker Walter White, played by Bryan Cranston, and Skyler White, played by Anna Gunn. In the show, the character was also known by the nickname "Flynn."

What made the casting so special was simple: like his character, Mitte lived with cerebral palsy. He did not have to pretend. He brought his own real-life experience into the role, and it showed in every scene — in the way the character moved, struggled, joked, got angry at his father and tried to hold his family together. Across the show's five seasons, from 2008 to 2013, Mitte appeared in 56 episodes, growing up on screen in front of the world.

*Breaking Bad* went on to become a television landmark, winning 16 Emmy Awards, including two for best drama series, and it is often called one of the greatest TV dramas ever made. In the middle of all that darkness and crime stood a young actor with crutches and a big heart, giving the story its most human face. For viewers who themselves live with disabilities, seeing him there — not hidden, not pitied, just a normal teenager — meant more than words can easily say.

It is worth pausing on how rare that was. When the show began in 2008, leading roles for actors with real disabilities were extremely hard to find. Mitte did not play a symbol or a lesson for other characters. He played a son — funny, stubborn, loving and sometimes furious, just like any teenager. Week after week, season after season, audiences simply accepted him as part of the family. That quiet normality was, in its own way, revolutionary, and it is a large part of why his performance is still talked about today.

## Life after Breaking Bad

Mitte's work did not end when the series finished. In 2014, he appeared in the series *Switched at Birth*. In 2021, he starred in the film *Triumph*, playing a high school wrestler with cerebral palsy — a role that once again drew on strength, discipline and determination. He was not only the star of that film; he was also one of its executive producers, helping to shape the story from behind the camera as well.

## More than an actor: his fight for others

If acting made RJ Mitte famous, advocacy made him important. He served as a spokesman for the Screen Actors Guild for performers with disabilities, speaking up for actors who are too often overlooked. He was a celebrity ambassador for United Cerebral Palsy and for Shriners Children's, two organisations that support people living with disabilities and children needing care. He was also president of the Mitte Foundation, which works to support the disabled community.

In 2013, his efforts were recognised with the SAG-AFTRA Harold Russell Award, an honour given for work that opens doors for performers with disabilities. Mitte understood something simple and powerful: when a person with a disability appears on screen as a full, real human being, it changes what millions of viewers believe is possible. He spent his career proving that point, again and again.

His advocacy was not a side project or a photo opportunity. It ran through everything he did — speaking for performers with disabilities, standing with United Cerebral Palsy and Shriners Children's, and leading his own foundation for the disabled community. Many actors use fame to be seen. Mitte used his to make sure others were seen too.

## "My sweet son": the tributes

The most emotional words came from the man who played his father. Bryan Cranston posted that he was in "deep despair" at the news. He called Mitte his "sweet son on Breaking Bad," and wrote that he loved him and would miss him. In his tribute message, Cranston also gently urged anyone who is struggling to seek support — in the US, help is available through the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.

Co-star Dean Norris said he was heartbroken, and remembered Mitte as "a sweetheart of a young man." It is a small phrase, but it says everything about how the people who knew him best saw him: kind, gentle and loved.

## A legacy bigger than one role

Thirty-four years is not a long life. But a life is not measured only in years. RJ Mitte will be remembered as Walter White Jr., of course — but also as a young man who took the very thing the world might have used to limit him, and turned it into his message. He showed that disability is not a lack of ability, and he opened a door that others will now walk through. He will be deeply missed.
